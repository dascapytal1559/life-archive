# The viewer and the console

The archive's own pages at /life-archive/.

## The viewer

The archive's own page, at `http://127.0.0.1:4650/life-archive/` (and on the tailnet at `/life-archive`, which Tailscale Serve passes on whole to the same path, so the captain's phone has it all, Health Record included; `/api/archive/` is never mounted there, and friend-map's page is at `/friend-map`, passed on whole to the same server the same way), is the console, at `/life-archive/dev` (see "The console"); any other address under `/life-archive/` (its root, or one of the old viewer's pages, retired by the captain's word, "can delete") opens the console's home, Events (`viewer/main.tsx`). Read-only but for Inspirations, which writes its own items and tags; friend-map is where people and events are changed. This section is the part of it that reads the archive's tables: the tables the console's Events aspect lists and opens (`viewer/Rows.tsx`), each a filter bar, a bar chart of its volume over time and a table of its rows, one filter object, kept in the page's address, driving all three; and the read API behind them.

What the tailnet reaches at `/life-archive`: the page's code (`viewer/`, the `src/` modules it imports, and its packages), the SDK (`sdk/`), every module's whole folder (`modules/`), its server half included, the viewer's read API (`/life-archive/api/`, metadata), the conversations a drawer opens when asked (`/life-archive/api/content/`, content, each read kept in the trail; see the drawer below) and the answers of the Health tab, rituals' two tabs and the Fitness page (`/life-archive/api/m/health`, `/life-archive/api/m/rituals` and its `/dashboard`, and `/life-archive/api/m/exercise/activities`, content, with the Bryan Johnson tab's `/life-archive/api/m/exercise/pillars`, its targets and habit statuses), and the Inspirations page's routes (`/life-archive/api/inspirations`, metadata; its writes guarded as "Inspirations" says). The tailnet's ACL should admit only the owner's own devices, and the door's Host guard still refuses any Host it does not admit. Vite serves files by path (`/life-archive/@fs/<path>`), so it is allowed `viewer/`, `src/`, `sdk/`, `modules/` and the packages alone, a module's folder whole, not just its page half, and denied `data/`, `life-archive-data/`, `.env*` and any `.sqlite` or `.sqlite3` file (rituals' store is one; `vite.config.ts`, held by `server/vite.test.ts`), so nothing of the archive's folder is ever served there.

- **Tables** (the console's Events, Tables, at `/life-archive/dev/events/tables`, the third tab after the Calendar and Daily rituals; called All, then Table, until the captain renamed it, and `/life-archive/dev/events/table` and `/life-archive/dev/events/all` still open it): a list of every table the archive keeps, grouped under whose each is (`tablesByOwner`: an owner's tables together, the owners in the order the server first names each, the tables of each in the server's order): under **Friend Map** its **Messages**, **Calls**, **Hangouts**, **People** and **Tags**; under **Life archive** first **Edits** (every edit to every table, with its Undo: "Editing" below), then the core's **LLM** and the LLM warehouse's **Prompts**, **Agent sessions** and **LLM tags** (events.md's "The LLM warehouse"); then the other modules' under their pages' names, exercise's **Workouts** under Fitness, rituals' **Rituals** and **Ritual days** under Rituals. Each row is a table: its name, what of it can be edited ("Edit: When, Hours, With, Note", the fields its owner lets be edited, in green, or "Read only"; `editsText`), how many rows it has and its columns. This list is the one place a table is shown and edited, and no module's page draws a table of its own: Friend Map had a **Messages** tab of its own from 7 to 9 November 2026, the same table at a second address, which the captain folded in here ("let's just fold friend maps messages and potentially even tables into the events page"). Its old address (`/life-archive/dev/friend-map/messages`, with whatever filters it carried) opens the Messages table here with its view as it was (`viewer/console/moved.ts`: a module says in its `viewer.tsx` where a place of its moved to), and Jump to finds every table by its name. A click opens that table exactly as it is kept, its own columns, filters, order, chart and drawer, as described next, with the way back to the list above it (the Tables tab goes back to it too); nothing mixes one table's rows into another's (the captain: "when you click on a table it shows that table exactly as it's kept ... At the moment I don't have any plans for like mixing table contents into one aggregated table"). The table open is named first in the address, its view after it (`/life-archive/dev/events/tables?table=calls&app=phone`). Each kind had a view of its own once; its old address (`/life-archive/dev/events/calls`) opens that table. A drawer's link to a table ("This chat", "Calls with Ada") opens it there, narrowed. The Calendar is the one view across tables; a card's drawer there has **Open in the Workouts table** (or whichever is its own), which opens the row's table on the Calendar's day the card is on (06:00 to 06:00, so the range names both dates; a row with no time on its own date) with its drawer open, since a row is changed in its table, never on the Calendar ("for editing it has to take us to the table page and allows us to edit it there"). The row opened and its drawer are always the one clicked; the rows around it follow the table's plain time range, so a row kept as a date alone (an untimed hangout or ritual) at the edge of the Calendar's 06:00 day may be listed with the next day or left out. Until 6 November 2026 this tab was one merged table of every kind by a toggle each (`show=`, Hangouts and Workouts on at first, `server/viewer/eventsTab.ts`), gone with this list; an address with `show=` opens the list.
- **Messages**: every message, one row each, with When, App, Chat (a one-to-one chat "with" its person, a group by its title, an untitled group by its first people, most messages there first), From (You, a person, or none for a notice), Type, Length (characters of its text, from `message_chars`, so the text itself is never read), Attachments ("2 images, video"), Reactions and Edited (or Unsent); filtered by app, chat kind (one-to-one, group, Marketplace), chat (a chat in a row narrows to it), who it is from (you, others, no one), type, a person (what they sent anywhere, and everything in their one-to-one chats) and time; charted as messages or characters.
- **Calls**: every call with anyone from every source, one row each (without their notes; Friend Map's own Table had a Calls tab until 10 November 2026), with When, With, App, Way, Answered, Length and From, filtered by app, answer, way, a person (a name in a row narrows to them) and time, and charted as calls or minutes. **Add call** in its head logs a call; a logged call's drawer has **Delete this call**.
- **Hangouts**: every hangout (`catch_up`), one row each, without its note: When (its date alone when it has no time; its instant is worked out as friend map pushes it, `hangoutRows` in `modules/friend-map/server/calendarRows.ts`; a trip's start, its end and private hours in the drawer), With (and how many more unnamed), Hours (a trip's in all) and People (how many were at it), each in person (a call is in Calls); filtered by with one person or several, a person and time; charted as hangouts or hours, split by how many. **Add hangout** in its head adds one; any hangout's drawer has **Delete this hangout**. Friend Map's own Table had a Hangouts tab until 10 November 2026.
- **LLM**: the conversations with an LLM, one row for each session, prompt and reply, from the calendar's rows (pushed from the event index): When, Kind, How (a session's way of running, a reply's start: a prompt or a notification), Project (its folder's name), Model, Characters (a session's are all its turns') and Length; filtered by kind, how, app, project, model and session (a session in a row narrows to it); charted as events or characters (the turns'), split by kind, how, model or project. Never the words: they stay in the session files.
- **Workouts**: the exercise module's tab (declared in its `module.ts`, so it goes with the module when that is switched off): the captain's activities from the calendar's workouts (the module's rows, pushed from its own index: Polar's, so far), one row each: When, Sport (in its source's word, "Road biking"), Distance, Length, Recorded by (the watch or app) and From (the source); filtered by sport family and recorder; charted as workouts or minutes, split by sport family or recorder. Copies of one activity from several sources (Polar's API and its export, say) are one row, matched as the Health tab matches them (one sport family, starts within two minutes, 80% of the shorter shared; `modules/exercise/server/workoutsTab.ts`), showing the copy the calendar had first, its drawer saying how many copies there are. Never a heart rate, a route or a sample.
- **People** (a table in Tables' list since 6 November 2026; edited here since 10 November 2026, when Friend Map's own Table folded in, the captain's "one table at a time, each once Events can do what Friend Map's does"): everyone the SQL views' `people` lists (anyone with an account, or edited; never you), one row each: Name, Tags, Location, Platforms, Accounts, Hidden (by you, or by which rule), Closeness (friend-map's own score, as the map views place people by it, at the closeness settings of friend-map's `settings.json`, with the rank among the people the filters leave but the search: `modules/friend-map/server/tabs/closeness.ts`), First and Last (their first and last message, call or hangout, ever), how many Messages (a one-to-one chat's both ways, and what they sent in groups; Marketplace, dropped chats and notices aside), Direct (the one-to-one ones), Calls and Hangouts there were, Added (when an import or he added them) and his Note for them (no content: his notes for people are metadata); found by any part of any of their names (the search box, `q`), filtered by tag (or no tag), platform and hidden, sorted by name, accounts, closeness, first, last, added or any count. Its drawer adds their other names, whether to reach out, their accounts and hours of hangouts, and **Their panel in Friend Map**, which opens the map with their panel open: what stays there is all a person's panel holds (merging, joining and detaching accounts, "this is me", their chat, a draft). **+ Add person** adds someone he knows offline (a name, and a location, tags and a note if he likes); when someone already goes by that name it says who, and **Add anyway** adds a second. Someone he added by hand, with no accounts, can be deleted from their drawer; anyone from his chats never is (hidden or joined instead). Friend Map's Table, its Location cell, Add person, the tag manager and the platform picker are gone; `/friend-map/table` (any tab) opens this table. The platform picker's counts of one platform alone are not here: the Platform filter narrows to the people on it, their counts still every platform's.
- **Tags** (friend map's tag table, friend-map's tag manager as a table, since 10 November 2026): every tag in his order, with its Order, Tag, how many People have it, whether it is Built in and when it was Made; sorted by its order first; found by name. **+ Add tag** makes one nobody has yet; a tag's Tag is renamed for everyone in its cell or drawer (onto a tag that exists, the two are merged, as the tag manager did after asking; Undo parts them again) and its Order moves it (1 is first); its drawer has **People with it** and **Delete this tag**, which takes it from everyone. The built-in tags (`archive`, `keep`, `live-together`, `show-name`) are moved but never renamed or deleted. A tag renamed here keeps its Hide switch on the map, as the tag manager carried it over (the console tells friend map's page half of the rename, `modules/friend-map/viewer/hide.ts`); merged into a tag that exists, the tag kept keeps its own. The chart counts the people active in each bucket (a message, call or hangout with them), or new in it (their first), split by hidden; a range narrows the table to those active in it and its counts to what was in it, and its totals count each person once. It works from the people view and an activity index, every hour each person had a message with you (one pass over `message_time` grouped by hour, cut at local midnight, chat and sender: under a second, then kept) with every call and hangout, worked out in memory by the hour (`modules/friend-map/server/tabs/peopleTab.ts`); after that a page takes about 20 ms and a chart under 70 ms.
- **The drawer**: a click on a row (or Enter on it) opens it at the right (from the bottom on a phone), kept in the address as `open`: every column under its name, what the table has no column for (a message's chat kind, the account it came from, its attachments' kinds and sizes; a hangout's start and unnamed others; an LLM event's app, tools used or pictures, session file, project folder and device; a person's aliases, accounts and hours of hangouts), and links to other tabs narrowed to it ("Messages that day", "This chat", "Calls with Ada", "This session", a person's messages, calls and hangouts). Metadata only, until he asks: a message's drawer has **Show the chat** and an LLM event's **Show the conversation** (`viewer/Conversation.tsx`), which open over the page (the whole screen on a phone) the chat around that message (the 20 before it, it and the 20 after; who said what, the days between, calls, reactions, attachments named by kind and never loaded) or the session the event is in (his prompts and the model's replies in order, each run of tool calls folded to its tools' names and opening to what each was given; from its start for a session, a couple of turns before the turn for a prompt or reply), 40 more at either end as it is scrolled there; ✕ or Escape goes back to the row. Nothing is read until the button is pressed, and its being open is not kept in the address, so a reload reads nothing. Each page is read only through the archive's content path (`ArchiveContent` for a chat, `readLlmContent` for a session, a page of turns at a time), on the archive's own connection, by `/life-archive/api/content/` (`server/viewer/content.ts`: `messages/:id/chat?before=|after=` and `llm/:event/session?offset=`), and kept in the trail of content reads first with the surface `viewer`: a chat as `conversation` from the message it was opened at, a session as `event-content` from the event it was opened at, each page one more read on that row. Who may ask is who may ask Health and Rituals: a Host the door admits, the viewer's own page, and on the tailnet only the devices its ACL admits (not the Inspirations writes' login check).
- **Editing** (`viewer/FieldEditor.tsx`, in a cell through `viewer/CellEdit.tsx` and in the drawer, `viewer/editWire.ts`, the server's `server/edits/`; the captain: "I would want to be able to edit tables directly from wherever I'm looking at a table", and the same table has the same permissions everywhere): a table whose owner lets its rows be edited (KindSpec's `edits`, declared beside the table; modules.md's "Editing a table") is edited in its cells and in the drawer, the same drawer whether it was opened from the Calendar or the table (asked "Edit in the grid, or keep editing in the drawer only?", the captain answered both, on 9 November 2026). **In a cell** (on a desk; on a phone the grid is only read and a tap opens the drawer): a column the table lets be edited shows a pen at its cell's right edge and an underline on hovering, and a click (or Enter on the cell) opens the drawer's own field editor over the cell, as wide as it needs; Enter or leaving it saves, Escape puts it back, and what was saved shows under the table with its **Undo**, as the drawer says it, the drawer open on that row reading it again. Which cells a row keeps as they are comes with each page of rows (`GET /life-archive/api/edits/locks?kind=&ids=`, one ask a page, never one a row); such a cell looks as it always did, its title saying why, and a click opens the drawer as on any cell only read. A note has no column, so it is the drawer's, as are what the table has no column for and the row's record. **In the drawer**: a value that can be edited shows a pen and a dotted underline on hovering; a click (or Enter) turns it into its editor (a date and a time in the archive's time zone, minutes, hours, a number in its unit, a choice, people found by name, a short text that may offer the table's own values as it is typed (a location), tags (chips, the table's offered as they are typed, Enter taking the first or what is typed as a new one), or a note); Enter or leaving it saves, Escape puts it back; under the fields, what was saved (with an **Undo**) or why it was not, and the row's record of edits (**Edits**), each with its own Undo while it stands. A value the captain corrected carries an "edited" mark, what its source said on hovering it, and a dot at its cell's corner in the table. A field this row keeps as it is (an imported call's time and people) looks as it always did, and hovering or tapping it says why; a field no table edits is never wrapped. A note is content: the drawer says whether there is one, and its words are read only when its editor opens, through the trail. Editable now: **Workouts** (sport, when it started and ended, distance, a note: each the captain's correction beside the copies Polar gave, so an import never undoes it), **Calls** (a call he logged: when, length, who and a note; an imported one: its note, whether it was answered, Yes, No or Not known over its source's word, and the length of a chat's call record whose app gives none), **Hangouts** (when, hours but a trip's, who, a note), **Ritual days** (each habit of a day done or not; a plank is its held time, logged through rituals), **Rituals** (a habit done set not done, which takes it from the table, its Undo bringing it back with its time), **People** (name, other names, tags, location, hidden, reach out, note: each through friend map's own write, as the person panel's) and **Tags** (name, order). Messages and the rest are only read. People and Tags are not in the calendar, so their owner finds their rows (TableEditor's `rows`; modules.md's "Editing a table"). Every edit is kept in the core's record (`npm run archive -- edits`, and `edits undo <n>`), and Tables' **Edits** (`console/EditsTable.tsx`, `/life-archive/dev/events/tables?table=edits`, narrowed to one table with `&of=calls`) lists it, the newest first, a hundred at a time with **Show older**: when, the table (a click opens the row in its table, its drawer open), what changed (a note's words never), where from, its number, and an **Undo** for each that stands, or what undid it. The record is one: for an owner whose own change log keeps its edits (friend map's, which its **Changes** can undo too, and exercise's), whether an edit stands undone is read from that log, so an edit undone from Friend Map's Changes reads "Undone in Friend Map's Changes" here and in the drawer, and its undo undone there makes it stand again; once the log has let a changeset go (after 90 days) the core's own record says. **Adding and deleting rows** (`viewer/AddRow.tsx`; asked "add and delete rows from a table?", the captain answered yes, for his own records only, on 9 November 2026): a table whose rows are partly his own records (KindSpec's `own`: Hangouts, and Calls for the calls he logged) has **+ Add hangout** or **+ Add call** at the right of its head, which opens a drawer for the new row in the row drawer's place, asking the fields its owner names (a call also what it was **On**: Their usual, or an app), with the field editor's own controls and today to start with; **Add** writes it through the one edit route, the table reads its rows again and the new row's drawer opens, and what was added shows under the table with its **Undo**. A row of his own has **Delete this hangout** (or call) at the foot of its drawer, asked once more; it leaves the table and its drawer says so, with an **Undo** that brings it back. An imported row is never deleted: its drawer says so and why. Both are in the record, as **Added hangout: …** and **Deleted call: …** in Edits and the row's own Edits, undone the same way (an add undone takes the row away again). They work on a phone too, in the drawer as it opens there.
- **The chart** has a bar for each local day, week (from Monday), month or year: the finest that gives at most 240 bars over the filtered rows' span, or the grain picked beside it. It can be split by a filter (Messages: app, chat kind, from or type; Calls: app, way or answered; and so on for each tab), each value a coloured part of the bar, named with its total (or the hovered bar's) in the key above; a value keeps its colour whatever the filters leave, and past eight they are added up as Other. Bucket edges are worked out in JavaScript (`src/time.ts`), so a day the clocks change is 23 or 25 hours. Dragging across bars narrows everything to their time, a click or tap on one bar to its bucket, and a double-click or the range's ✕ clears it.
- **The table** is the server's: it sorts and pages the rows (100 at a time, as they scroll into view); the page draws only those on screen, all one height, the header held at the top and, on a phone, the first column at the left.
- **Speed on over a million messages** (`modules/friend-map/server/tabs/messagesTab.ts`): every Messages filter is a condition on the `message_time` index's columns, so nothing reads a message's row until a page is drawn; a page after another starts right after its last row (`after`, the answer's `next`), however deep, and a jump down the scrollbar skips whole months by their counts; the chart counts each bucket over its own stretch of the index; the filters' counts come from one grouping of the time range by chat, sender and type, kept and counted in memory. Answers are kept until the archive's people, links, messages or chats change, or ten minutes pass. On a made-up archive of 1.39 million messages the all-time chart takes about 30 ms (split, up to about 300 ms), a first page under 50 ms, a page after another under 1 ms and a jump to any depth under 65 ms; the filters' counts take about 400 ms the first time for all time, then a few milliseconds.
- **Privacy.** The viewer's queries run on connections of its own (`server/viewer/connection.ts`), every one read-only and `query_only`: the core's, the calendar's, and one for each module's database, opened as the module says (its `database.reads`). Its connection to friend map's archive (`openForQuery` in `modules/friend-map/server/sqlRead.ts` with one function more, `wall_clock`), for the Messages and People tabs and friend map's answers, has the SQL runner's authorizer, so a query touching a message's text, a raw record, a note or a draft fails instead of reading it. Its connection to the calendar (`openCalendarReads`), for the tabs of events and the day summaries, has none, because the calendar holds no content, and its queries are the tabs' own fixed SELECTs. The tests run every tab, filter, split, measure and page through these connections and check that no answer carries a text or a note; only the archive's would refuse a query that touched one. A message's length is the archive's `message_chars` (a size, which the privacy policy counts as metadata), kept by triggers as messages come, change and go. Nothing is written, but by the Inspirations page, its own items and tags alone (see features.md's "Inspirations"), and the trail's rows for the conversations a drawer opens: changes to people stay in friend-map. Those conversations are the one way the viewer reads content, and they read it on the archive's own connection through its content path, never on the viewer's two.
- **A new tab** is one definition in `tabsWith` (`server/viewer/tabs.ts`), or a module's, declared in its `module.ts` (`tabs`, as Workouts is exercise's; `docs/modules.md`): its spec (columns, filters, measures, splits, what it sorts by) and how it answers for its rows, chart and filters' counts. A tab of events is one SELECT over the calendar's rows (`server/calendar/`; "The calendar") with a SQL expression for each filter, and its drawer that SELECT's row with what the rows' owner adds (`sourceTab.ts`, as Calls, Hangouts, LLM, Workouts and Rituals are; its rows are keyed by the calendar's own ids); a big one over the archive plans its own queries (as Messages and People do). Each also answers for one row (`detail`), for its drawer. A tab over the calendar gives each row its time (`at`), for the Calendar. Every tab is listed in Events' Tables, the core's first and then the modules' (`moduleTabs` stamps each with its module's id, `module`, so the list says whose it is).

Its read API, under `/life-archive/api/` (`server/viewer/http.ts`): `GET kinds` (the tabs), and for each tab `GET <kind>/rows` (`offset`, `limit`, `sort`, `dir`, `after`), `<kind>/volume` (`grain`, `measure`, `split`, and `begins`, HH:MM, for days that begin later than midnight, as the Events calendar's do at 06:00; a row kept as a date alone, a hangout with no time, then counts on its own date) `<kind>/facets` (each filter's values with how many rows have each under the other filters) and `<kind>/row?id=` (one row, for its drawer), all taking the filters as `from` and `to` (ms), `person`, `q` (a search) and each choice filter's values (a row's id is its table's own: a call's is the calendar's own id). And `GET calendar/days?from=&to=&person=` (`server/viewer/calendarDays.ts`): the calendar's day summaries over a range of days, a day each, how many messages, whom he messaged (named by the owner) and how many messages he sent agents, and how its rituals went, which the Events calendar's page reads for its Messaged, Agents and Rituals rows, its month's tints and its year. Each table is asked by its own routes (`calls/rows`), from its page in Tables and from the Calendar. To try it without real data: `npm run testing-data -- <new folder> --crowd` (the made-up crowd, with made-up messages, calls, hangouts and LLM sessions) and the server on it on a spare port, as "Running" says.

## The console

The console, at `/life-archive/dev` (on this Mac and on the tailnet, the captain's phone included), is the life archive's front end: P3, "Console", of the October 2026 worlds round, which the captain chose as "a generic but premium menu console" to house every view, "a frame to attach all of our future design explorations to". Its rail is the captain's: Events first, the home since 6 November 2026 ("events is almost gonna become like home page for life archive"), then Friend Map, Health Record, Fitness and Inspirations, every page of the old viewer but People, which Friend Map replaces ("That's just the best view I wanna have on my social state"), and Architecture, removed on 2 November 2026 (see wiring.md's "Architecture"), with Shopping last, and after it any private module's page. The old viewer itself is gone ("can delete"): the page at `/life-archive/` is the console alone, and any address of the old viewer's opens its home, Events. The code is `viewer/console/`, with the pages it wraps beside it in `viewer/`.

Its address was `/life-archive/console` until 8 November 2026, when the captain asked for two builds side by side ("/console can be removed and in its place /dev and /main"): `/life-archive/dev` is this page, the dev branch's, on his archive, his daily driver; `/life-archive/main` is the main branch's preview, the same console as main has it over the demo's made-up data, plain files with no server behind them (demo.md's "The main preview"). The server sends any address of the console's under the old one on to the same place under `/life-archive/dev`, its query kept (302; `address.ts`'s `fromFormerConsole`), so bookmarks and the notes that link there still open; the console's own source files, which the page's Vite serves at `/life-archive/console/...` as it runs, are left to it.

- **The shell** (`Console.tsx`, `console.css`, and on a phone `phone.css`): a rail at the left and the surface beside it, the whole height of the screen with no bar or caption above it, drawn with the console's blocks. The rail holds the aspects, its pages (no heading over them, and no Overview page since 3 November 2026: "We can remove the overview page, we can remove aspects as a label, and then we can remove default as an ordering toggle"), one level and never anything under them (the captain's word: "keep the left nav nav menu single items ... each page gets to have its own like tabs"); at its foot Jump to and the settings gear (`Settings.tsx`). A page with more than one view has its own tabs along the top of its surface, one a view in the registry's order, the current one underlined (a page that fills the area has them in a strip above it; on a phone they scroll sideways); a page of one view has none. Each tab keeps its view's address, so links and Jump to land on it as before. Premium white by day and true black at night: the settings gear opens a small menu above it whose one setting is the look, three choices, Automatic (follows the device), Light and Dark, the current one checked (the captain, 9 November 2026: "change the light dark mode selector into a settings icon for now"); a choice applies at once and closes the menu, and Esc, Tab or a click beside it closes it, Esc and Tab back on the gear; Light and Dark are kept on this device (`localStorage`, `life-archive.console.theme`), and `?theme=light|dark` on an address forces one for that page (a review board's frames use it); the browser's own colour around the page follows. The device's own faces (SF Pro and SF Mono on a Mac or an iPhone; Inter and a mono face elsewhere), one signal-green accent, tabular numerals, no web fonts shipped. On a phone (under 900 px) the console is laid out from the bottom up (the captain's "bottom driven design", 8 November 2026): a bar along the foot with the life archive's mark and name at the left (a tap opens the home) and, at the right, the current page's icon and name, which opens the rail as a dropdown rising over the surface (every page, Jump to and the settings gear, whose menu Esc closes alone, leaving the dropdown open; Esc, a tap beside it or opening a place closes it); above the bar the page's tabs in one row, the current one marked along its top edge, scrolling sideways when they do not fit; and the surface above them, scrolling while the bar and the tabs stay put, the bar clear of the iPhone's home indicator.
