# People

How everyone in the archive is kept as a person: names, aliases, tags, links and merges, and how to change them from a chat.

## People

People are the friend map module's (`modules/friend-map/`, its README): they live in its archive, `modules/friend-map/archive.sqlite` in the archive's folder, and are changed through friend-map's page (a person's panel), the Events page's People and Tags tables, or `npm run archive -- person ...` and the other people commands, which the archive's CLI hands to the module (`modules/friend-map/server/commands.ts`), as before. Two tables in the archive hold who is who, two more hold the tags, and a column of the person table the location:

- **Import people** (`import_person`; the API and the connectors still call one an account): one identity on one platform, as an import found it. Its platform, its key (a WhatsApp JID, a phone number or email for iMessage, a display name for Messenger and Instagram, a numeric user id for X, an ACI for Signal, a user id for Discord), its handle, the name the import gave it and where the connector found that name (`name_source`: the address book, a chat, a push name, the export...), and when imports first and last reported it. Each points at one person, and says how that link was made (`link_source`) and when:
  - `import`: an import found the identity and made a person for it;
  - `rule:same-name`: the rules joined it with someone of exactly the same name (below);
  - `rule:same-number`: a call account (platform `phone`, from the call history) the rules put on the person who holds the same number or Apple ID as an iMessage account (see archive.md's "Calls" under archive.md's "The message archive");
  - `captain`: you linked it: a merge, a confirmed proposal, "Not them", `person link`, or an account a person file listed before the files moved in.
- **People** (`person`): your record of someone: their names (see "Names" below: their name, which labels them everywhere, then their aliases, in order), tags (below), a location (below), a note (free text; see "Notes" below), whether you should reach out to them (see "Reaching out" below), the messages you are drafting to them (see "Drafts" below), the seed their identicon is drawn from and the colour you picked for it (see "Identicons" below), and the archive state the rules stored for them. You edit any of these for anyone, in their panel or with the CLI, with nothing to create first. **Adding someone by hand**, someone you know offline with no messages, so you can give them a location, tags, a note, hangouts and calls: **+ Add person** in Events' People table (console.md's "People") takes a name and, if you like, a location (the values in use offered), tags and a note; Enter or Add adds them with no accounts, as one edit in the record that Undo takes away again, and opens their drawer. They are edited by hand, so the archive rules never archive them for having no messages: they are in Events' People table, People and SQL at once, and on Orbits in the outer belt until a hangout or a call gives them closeness. Before adding anyone, it looks for someone who already goes by that name or an alias, as the rules compare names but for case (`People.sameNamed` in `modules/friend-map/server/people.ts`): if there is, nobody is added yet, and the form says who, or adds a second all the same with "Add anyway" (a hand-made person and an import of one name of two words or more are joined by the rules at the next import, into the hand-made one). `POST /api/archive/people` does it (`anyway` adds all the same), and `person new` on the CLI, which refuses such a name unless given `--anyway`. Neither is there in the demo. A person the import made follows its account's name exactly as the platform wrote it, stray spaces and all (`Haruki  Oda `), until you rename them. An account's name is the one its newest source gives, except that a stand-in (a placeholder such as "WhatsApp user", or only a number) never replaces a name a source found, so live WhatsApp, which may know someone only by their number, leaves them the name your address book gave an import (`isStandIn` in `modules/friend-map/server/write.ts`).
- **Tags** (`tag`, and `person_tag` for who has which, with when it was added; `modules/friend-map/server/tags.ts` is the only code that touches them): one row per tag, with its name, its place in your order and when it was made. A tag is there whether or not anyone has it, so Events' Tags table lists it and the person panel's suggestions offer it all the same, in your order (the map's Hide lists the tags its query's matches have, those Hide starts on, and any switched on); a person's tags are always listed in that order too. Taking a tag from the last person who has it (in their panel, with `person untag` or `person tags`, or with `edit untag`, whose dry run says so) deletes the tag too, in the same changeset, so one undo brings back both, the tag in its place: a tag made by mistake goes once nobody has it. A new tag never takes the number of one the change log remembers, so that undo works even after the right tag is made. A tag nobody had before (one just added in Events' Tags for later) stays, as do `archive`, `keep` and `commercial` (the tags Hide starts on, `HIDDEN_BY_DEFAULT`). Renaming or deleting a tag is one change everyone with it follows: renaming it to a tag that already exists merges the two (everyone with either has the one kept, which keeps its place, and the other is gone), and deleting one takes it from everyone. Giving someone a tag that isn't in the table yet (in their panel, say) adds it, last. `archive`, `keep`, `live-together` (see "How closeness is scored") and `show-name` (whom the name choice No name except… names; see "Orbits" under console.md's "The views") are built in: always there, and never renamed, merged into or deleted, though they can be moved; everyone the rules archive shows `archive` too, though it is never stored for them (below), and `system-archive`, its old name, is refused as a tag. Merging two people gives the one kept both's tags, but for `archive` and `keep`: the one kept keeps its own archive state. Tags moved into these tables from each person's own list (`person.tags_json`, which is gone) in migration 9 in `server/archive/db.ts`: your quick tag defaults of the time first, then every other tag A to Z, `archive` and `keep` among them, each with the people who had it; a new, empty archive starts with just `archive` and `keep`, and migration 31 adds `live-together` after them, and migration 48 `show-name` after those (or, where you had already made a tag of either name, keeps it, with its people and its place, and makes it built in).
- **Location** (`person.location`, one value or none; `modules/friend-map/server/locations.ts` reads it across people): where someone is. The convention is their **last known location**: wherever you last knew them to be (`Sampletown`), so it is changed when they move rather than kept as history. The person panel and Events' People table's Location offer the locations people are at, the most people first, or take a new one. A location is written as you type it, trimmed and single-spaced (letters or digits, up to 60 characters), and locations that differ only in case are one: the column compares without case, and one someone is already at is written in their spelling (`sampletown` is `Sampletown`), so each is written one way (`npm run check-data` names any written two ways). The column is indexed, so who is at a location and every location with how many (`npm run archive -- locations`, `GET /api/archive/locations`) come from the index alone. Renaming one is one change everyone at it follows, and renaming it onto another location someone is at merges the two (everyone at either is at the one kept): `npm run archive -- location rename <location> <new name>` (the server's `PUT /api/archive/locations/:name`); the app does not offer it. To take one from everyone, `edit clear-location --query 'location:<location>'`. Setting it marks the person edited, and the captain's own person has none. Merging two people keeps the kept one's location, and puts a different one of the other's into its note (`Location: Sampletown`); without one, the kept person takes the other's. Location began as the `met-` and `lives-` tags you had started: migration 14 in `server/archive/db.ts` made each such tag a value of a field of its own (tables `field_value` and `person_field_value`) named from the rest of the tag, each word capitalised (`met-new-york` is `New York`), gave it to everyone who had the tag (someone with several `met-` tags got the first in your order of the time, then any other A to Z), and then took the tags from everyone and deleted them, as `tag delete` does. There was also an occupation field (what they do, any number of values), dropped because tags say it as well: migration 15 deleted its values and who had them, and narrowed the fields to met (where you met someone) and lives (where they live). Migration 27 made those two one field, Location: everyone kept their lives value, anyone without one took their met value (as the lives value of that name, but for case, if there was one), and met and its values went, so someone with both kept where they lived; it keeps what moved in the archive (`meta` `location_fold`). Migration 32 made Location this column: everyone kept their value, the two tables went, and so did values nobody had; the change log's rows of them became person rows holding the location, so older changesets still undo (one that set a met value is refused). It keeps how many people had a location and how many different ones, before and after, in `meta` `location_column`. `npm run archive -- migrate` (a backup first, when the schema is behind) prints both, naming everyone whose different met place went. `met:` and `lives:` in a query, and the CLI's `met` and `lives` commands, now say so and point at location. The People search and `people --query` find people by their location, `people --location` (and `GET /api/archive/people?location=`) lists exactly those with one, but for case, and Events' People table has a column for it.

Clicking someone gathers everything from all the import people that point at them: messages, chats, counts, the timeline. Loading is a join (each person, their import people, and each import person's counts, which are kept in `import_person_stats`); nothing is worked out again when the page or the archive loads. The map's report is kept too (`derived`), and made again only when links or messages change.

**Links are stored, and the rules only fill in what nobody chose.** The rules run after every import, when the code's rules change (the first open after an upgrade), and on `npm run archive -- rules`; never on a page load or a server start otherwise. They:

- give every new import person a person of its own (your own accounts go to you), and anyone without an identicon seed theirs (see "Identicons");
- count what each import person did, from the messages (the one scan of every message, a second or two on a big archive);
- join people with exactly the same name, and store which rule archives each person and why (see archive.md's "Who is archived");
- propose people who may be the same.

People with exactly the same name are joined into one person, on any platforms, when the name is two words or more or written in Chinese characters (Messenger `Kit Ng`, WhatsApp `Kit Ng` and iMessage `Kit Ng` are one person, as are two WhatsApp numbers both named `周伟`). A lone word such as `Cleo` or `Hugo` is too common for that, so it is proposed instead. The same means character for character: only what cannot be seen is ignored (format and direction marks such as a right-to-left mark, a no-break space for a space, spaces at either end, an accent stored as one character or two), so `Kit Ng` / `KIT NG` and `Cleo Example` / `Cléo Example` are not joined. A person's names are their own and each of their accounts' names, so an account you linked to someone you renamed still joins anyone with that account's name, and a chain of shared names is one person. People the archive rules archived are joined too (a Signal account seen only in group chats with the full name of someone you message on WhatsApp, say), and the joined person is then archived or shown by the rules as anyone else; never you, anyone you archived yourself (the tag `archive`), or placeholder names such as "Facebook user". A join never moves a link you made, and never folds away someone you have edited (named, given an alias or tag, merged or added by hand) or linked an account to: such a person keeps their person and gains the others, and two such people with one name are proposed instead. A joined person's panel says so and why.

A few other signs that two people are one are proposed, and you decide in the People panel ("Same person?", shown only when something is waiting) or with the CLI: the same phone number, the same one-word name (`Cleo` / `Cleo`), one name whose words (two or more) are all in the other (`Rowan Quill` / `Rowan Ryan Quill`), and exactly the same name when it is not joined (two people you have edited, or someone you said is different from another with that name); a pair with the same name of two words or more is joined rather than proposed, whatever else they share. Names are compared only between people on the map; the same phone number is proposed even when the archive rules have archived one side or both (a Signal account seen only in group chats, say, with the number of someone you message on WhatsApp), but never for someone you archived yourself (the tag `archive`). Confirming such a proposal merges them as any other, and a merged person counts as edited, so is shown. Names that only look alike are not proposed: a different case, accents or punctuation (`Kit Ng` / `KIT NG`), a lone first name (`Ada` / `Ada Sample`), or the same first name and last initial (`Bruno P` / `Bruno Placeholder`); join those yourself with "Join person" in the person panel.

**Merging** two people keeps one and deletes the other: the other's import people point at the one kept (linked by you), its aliases and tags are added to the kept person's (no repeats; not `archive` or `keep`, so the kept person stays archived or shown as it was, by its own tags), its note to the kept person's note (after a blank line, unless one already holds the other), its drafts, hangouts and logged calls are the kept person's, so are the group chats it was unlinked from (see archive.md's "Unlinking someone from a group chat"), the kept person is to be reached out to if either was, it takes the other's location where it has none (where it has another, the other's goes into its note as a line, `Location: Sampletown`, added as notes are), and the kept person's name and identicon stay. A join by the rules carries notes, drafts, reaching out, location, hangouts and logged calls over the same way, and "This is me" notes. Confirming a proposal keeps the person you have edited, else the one with more messages. **"Join person"** in a panel (at the right of the Accounts heading) joins the person whose panel it is into someone you pick by name or alias (archived people too, listed after everyone else), from anyone's panel, archived or not, a deleted or unknown account's included (an "Instagram User" you know is a friend, say; the account then stays theirs, linked by you, through every later import): after a one-line "Join A into B?", B is kept with its name and the panel shows B, and A is deleted. Its box, like People's and Orbits' find box, finds what you type anywhere in a name, whatever its case or width (full-width "ＲＹＡＮ" is "ryan"), Chinese, Japanese and Korean included: one such character is enough (a surname such as 周, where Latin needs two letters), spaces between such characters do not count ("周 伟" is found as 周伟), and a Korean syllable still being typed finds the one it becomes (`holds` and `enoughToSearch` in `src/names.ts`). **"Not them"**, the × beside an account (`npm run archive -- detach`), takes it out into a fresh person of its own (with an identicon of its own), linked by you, and remembers that the two are different. People you have said are different (a rejected proposal, or "Not them") are never joined or proposed again, even through a third person with the same name; the others with that name still are. **"This is me"** (`npm run archive -- person me`) gives a person's accounts to you (an old name of yours, say): their messages count as yours. **Deleting** a person (`npm run archive -- person delete`) works only for someone with no accounts left, such as a person added by hand; anyone with an account is joined or split instead, and you are never deleted.

The page, Events' People and Tags tables (console.md's "People" and "Tags": a person's fields, Add person, and the old tag manager's add, rename, delete and order, as tables) and the CLI change the same tables, and an open page follows changes made anywhere, the CLI included (`GET /api/archive/events`). Every edit to people, from either, is one changeset in the change log, which shows each person's before and after and can undo it (see "Changing data from a chat"). When only new messages came in (a live chat, with nobody new, renamed, joined or archived by the rules), the server says so with a lighter event, and only what shows counts reloads: the map and the open person's panel, whose chat adds the new messages at the bottom and keeps every page it has loaded. People's list takes the new counts at the next change to people, or when opened again. `npm run archive` with no command lists everything:

```sh
npm run archive -- people [--tag climbing]      # everyone, most recent first, each with their number
npm run archive -- people --reach-out           # those you should reach out to, each with their drafts
npm run archive -- people --location sampletown  # those last known to be there, archived or not
npm run archive -- person show <person>         # name, aliases, tags, location, note, reaching out, and each account with its number and link
npm run archive -- person set-name <person> <name ...>    # their name, which labels them; one of their aliases moves first, as a drag does
npm run archive -- person add-alias <person> <alias ...>
npm run archive -- person remove-alias <person> <alias ...>
npm run archive -- person tag <person> <tag ...>
npm run archive -- person untag <person> <tag ...>
npm run archive -- tag list                     # every tag, in order, with how many have it
npm run archive -- tag add <name>               # a new tag, last
npm run archive -- tag rename <tag> <new name>  # for everyone; onto a tag that exists, the two are merged
npm run archive -- tag delete <tag>             # take it from everyone
npm run archive -- tag move <tag> <position>    # put it at this place in the order (1 first)
npm run archive -- person location <person> <value ...>     # where they are, as far as you last knew (one value); clear-location takes it away
npm run archive -- locations                     # every location someone is at, the most people first, with how many
npm run archive -- location rename Smpl Sampletown  # for everyone at it; onto another location someone is at, the two are merged
npm run archive -- person link <account> <person>   # this account is this person (your link)
npm run archive -- person unlink <account>          # the account becomes a person of its own ("Not them")
npm run archive -- person new <name ...> [--location Hobart] [--tag friend,climbing] [--note "..."]  # add someone by hand, with no accounts yet; refused, naming them, when someone already goes by that name (--anyway adds them all the same)
npm run archive -- person me <person>               # "This is me"
npm run archive -- person delete <person>           # delete someone with no accounts left (never you)
npm run archive -- person note <person> <text ...>  # set their note ("line one\nline two" in quotes keeps lines)
npm run archive -- person clear-note <person>
npm run archive -- person reach-out <person>        # you should reach out to them (Orbits' glow)
npm run archive -- person no-reach-out <person>
npm run archive -- person draft <person> <text ...>  # set the message you are writing to them (their first draft; no changeset)
npm run archive -- person clear-draft <person>
npm run archive -- contact-notes <address book>  # fill notes from the contacts (below)
npm run archive -- proposals                    # possible same-person pairs
npm run archive -- confirm <#> | reject <#>
npm run archive -- merge <person> <person>      # the second into the first, without a proposal
npm run archive -- detach <person> <account>    # "Not them"
npm run archive -- tags <person> sibling archive  # set every tag at once
npm run archive -- groups <words>               # group chats by title, each with its number (for a query's group:#n)
npm run archive -- group drop <#>               # a group that links nobody (a bulk message); group restore <#>, groups --dropped
npm run archive -- group remove-member <#> <person>  # someone added by mistake who never wrote there; group put-back, groups --removed
npm run archive -- catch-ups [--person <person>]  # catch-ups, the latest first, each with its number
npm run archive -- calls [<person>]             # everyone's calls, the most time on answered calls first; or one person's
npm run archive -- catch-up add 12 34 --day 2026-10-04 --time 19:30 --hours 2.5 --note "dinner"  # time in person: today, no time, an hour, unless said
npm run archive -- catch-up change 7 --hours 1 --people 12  # what is given; catch-up delete 7
npm run archive -- catch-up add 12 34 --day 2026-09-04 --time 18:00 --until 2026-09-06 --end 16:00  # a trip: its hours worked out; --private 23:00-07:00 for other private hours
npm run archive -- catch-up change 7 --one-day --hours 3  # a trip back to one day
npm run archive -- call add 12 --day 2026-10-04 --time 19:30 --minutes 1:30 --account 40  # log a call: today unless said, on the account they are called on unless said
npm run archive -- call add 12 34 --time 20:00 --minutes 45 --account 41 --others 2  # a call with several people: each on their usual account, unless an --account of theirs says; --others for those unnamed
npm run archive -- call change 3 --minutes 25   # what is given (--person, --day, --time, --minutes, --account, --others); call delete 3
npm run archive -- edit tag commercial --query 'group:#75 AND untagged'   # many people at once: a dry run
npm run archive -- changes                      # the change log; changes show <#>, changes undo <#>
npm run archive -- rules                        # run the rules now
npm run archive -- connectors                   # on or off, last run, last error
npm run archive -- timeline <person>            # prints message text, kept in the trail of content reads
npm run archive -- search <words>               # prints message text, kept in the trail of content reads
npm run archive -- timeline <person> --why "<the captain's words>"   # with the job's reason, kept with the read
npm run archive -- timeline 2 --testing --json  # the same over the testing data, never the archive (Ada Sample is 2)
npm run archive -- search <words> --redacted    # every text as its length; --json for the app's own answer
npm run archive -- content-log                  # the trail: every content read, the latest first
```

Names are trimmed and single-spaced; aliases are names, without repeats; tags are lowercase words or hyphenated phrases (letters, digits and dashes, up to 40): spaces become dashes wherever a tag is typed, so `Sampletown era` is `sampletown-era`, and `people --tag` finds it either way; `me` is not a tag ("This is me" says that). Tags mean nothing to the app except `archive`, which archives the person (off the map and every view), and `keep`, which stops the automatic archive rules archiving them (see archive.md's "Who is archived"); everyone the automatic rules archive, whichever rule, shows `archive` too, in the person panel (without a remove button), Hide and queries, worked out from the rule and never stored (one archive: their panel says whether you or a rule archived them, and which rule); `system-archive`, the old name for the rules' archive, can't be set as a tag; `friend` is an ordinary tag, and so is `hide`, which archived people long ago.

**Names.** Everyone goes by one name or more, kept as one list in your order: the first is their name (`person.name`), which labels them everywhere (the map, People, the Events People table, the hover card, a group chat's members), and the rest are their aliases (`person.aliases_json`), each once, never their name again. The person panel shows them as a view more than a form (`src/NameChips.tsx`, the list's changes in `src/nameChips.ts`): their round identicon (which opens its picker), as tall as the two rows beside it (the Name line and one row of chips) and staying that size at the top when the chips wrap,: the **Names +** label, one button with only its **+** green, which turns into a borderless **add new name** box in its place (Enter, or leaving it with something typed, adds it as the last name and puts the label back; Esc, or leaving it empty, puts it back with nothing added), and below it a chip for each name, the first bolder. Drag a chip to another place, by mouse or by touch, and dropping one first makes it their name, the name before it staying as an alias; with the keyboard, focus a chip and move it with Alt and an arrow key (the arrows alone go from chip to chip). Pointing at a chip or focusing it shows its × to remove it, but on the last name left, as nobody is without a name; a click or tap that does not drag, or Enter, writes it again in place, their name as much as an alias (Enter or leaving it saves, Esc or leaving it empty puts it back; written as another of their names, the two are one). Each change saves every name at once (`PUT /api/archive/people/:id/names`, `People.setNames`) as one changeset, whose chip offers Undo and which the change log shows. A name made first by you is yours (`name_source` `captain`), so it no longer follows an import's name; adding or reordering aliases behind an import's name leaves it following. The map's own name choices (First name, First L.) leave the chips in full, as they are kept; with No name except… they show neutral shapes (a picked person's name alone), and they are not changed until another choice is back; a read-only page shows the names alone (as First L. in the demo). On the CLI, `person set-name` makes one of their aliases their name as a drag would (another name takes their name's place), and `add-alias` and `remove-alias` change the rest. Every name is searchable (People, Events' People table, the find box, Join person), and the Query bar's SQL has every name in the view `person_names`, one row each, so a person can be found by any of the names they go by. Until migration 42 the first alias was the label, with the name beneath; that migration made everyone's first alias their name (and the change log's copies of them, so older changes still undo), so each kept the label they had, and `npm run archive -- migrate` says how many.

**Notes.** A note is free text about someone, of any lines (Windows line ends become plain ones, space at either end is trimmed, up to 10,000 characters). You write it in their panel (saved when you leave the box) or with `person note`; the People search and `people --query` find people by it, and No name except… (see "Orbits" under console.md's "The views") hides it. Notes can also come from the address book (the Mac's Contacts, as `npm run mac-imports` copies them into `platform-exports/addressbook/<day>/`, or an iPhone's `AddressBook.sqlitedb`): each contact card's company, department and job title (`Company: …`, `Department: …`, `Job title: …`; a company's own card's name is not a company) and then its note become the note of every person one of the card's phone numbers or emails belongs to, matched as the connectors match them to name accounts; a person on several cards gets each once, a blank line between. Your own card writes nothing. This runs after a `whatsapp-db` or `imessage-db` import given `--contacts`, and on `npm run archive -- contact-notes <address book>`. It writes only notes that are empty or still exactly as it last wrote them (`person.address_book_note`), so running it again brings those up to date, and a note you have changed (or written) is never written over; the panel says when a note is still the address book's. A card with neither company nor note changes nothing. The address book is read from a copy and never written.

**Reaching out.** **Should reach out** (`person.should_reach_out`, a switch) is in their panel below Note, beside Archive, and on the CLI (`person reach-out`, `no-reach-out`); setting it marks the person edited. Everyone you should reach out to has Orbits' glow, and `people --reach-out` (or `GET /api/archive/people?reachOut=1`) lists them, with their drafts, read from an index on the flag. The switch stays with No name except…, since the glow shows it anyway.

**Drafts.** At the foot of their panel, under their chat (where Send on Telegram was: sending from friend-map is off for now, see archive.md's "Telegram, live"), a **Draft** box holds the message you are writing to them, to rework as often as you like. It saves as you type (a moment after you stop, or when you leave the box or the panel; `src/drafts.ts`) and is there again when the panel opens, saying Saved, Saving… or why it did not save. A draft is your scratch space, not an edit: it makes no changeset, marks nobody edited and tells no open page to load again. Drafts are kept in the archive (`draft`: whom it is to, its text, written as a note is, and when it was made and last changed; `modules/friend-map/server/drafts.ts` is the only code that touches them), any number per person, though the panel offers one: it shows each one someone has (a merge can give someone two) and one empty box for someone with none. A draft emptied is deleted. They follow a merge or join to the person kept (logged with the merge, so undoing it takes them back) and go with a person deleted. No name except… hides them, as it hides notes; a read-only page shows a draft there is and offers none to write. `person draft` and `clear-draft` on the CLI set or delete someone's first draft, and `npm run check-data` checks they are written as the app writes them. Nothing writes a draft for you. They began as the **draft opener**, a field of the person (`person.draft_opener`): migration 38 in `server/archive/db.ts` made each opener its person's first draft and dropped the column, keeping how many moved (`meta` `drafts_from_openers`, which `npm run archive -- migrate` tells); an older changeset that only set an opener has nothing left to put back. The flag took over from the `should-call` tag: migration 13 in `server/archive/db.ts` flagged everyone who had it and then deleted the tag, as `tag delete` does.

**Hangouts.** Time you had with someone in person that no message shows is a hangout (a catch-up in the code, the archive and the CLI; every word the page uses for one comes from `src/eventWords.ts`, so it can be named again in one line): a date (today or before), when it started if you like (a **time** of day local time, such as 7:30 pm: shown with it and where it sits in their chat, never part of the score), how many **hours** it lasted (any number above 0 and at most 24, such as 1.5, kept to two decimal places), **in person** (a call is logged as a call, "Logged calls" below; there is no hangout by call, "Hangouts by call" below), a short note if you like, one person or more, never you (a wedding is one hangout with everyone you saw there), and how many **others** were there that you do not name (a whole number, 0 unless said: a crowd you did not really catch up with, say 20). Ticking **Several days** makes it a **trip** away together (a holiday with friends, say): it asks when it **Started** and when it **Ended**, each a date and a time (when you left and when you got home: its date and time, which a trip needs, to its **last day** at its **end time**), and the hours each day you kept to yourself, **Private each day**, from one time to another (midnight to 10 am to start; a window across midnight, 11 pm to 7 am, works too, and the same time twice is none). Its time together is every hour from its start to its end outside that window, each day's part on its own date: leaving at 6 pm and home at 2 pm two days later is 6 hours, then 14, then 4, 24 in all; the form says how many days and hours it comes to. A trip is one hangout, at most 366 days, in person: listed, added, changed, deleted and undone whole, its hours its time together in all, which the archive works out (`tripHours` in `src/catchUps.ts`). In friend-map's closeness score its first 8 hours count whole and the rest less and less (`tripFullHours`; see friend-map's "How closeness is scored"). Each counts toward their closeness by its hours, each one at it taking their share, the unnamed others counted too (see "How closeness is scored"). Add one in someone's panel with **Add event**, beside "Hangouts", or "+ event" on a day of their chat (Events' Calendar has **+ Hangout** and **+ Call** on a day instead; console.md's "Calendar"): its toggle at the top picks **Call** or **Hangout** (Message joins them once there is a form for one: `MESSAGE_EVENTS` in `src/eventWords.ts`), Hangout to start, and the fields below follow it. **Add hangout** in the Hangouts table on the Events page's Tables adds one too, from its When (a date, and the time it started if you like), Hours, With and Note, and **Delete this hangout** at the foot of a row's drawer deletes one, asked once more; each is an edit in the tables' record (Events' Edits) as well as a changeset, undone from either. A trip and unnamed others are added and changed in the form. A hangout starts with today, no time, 1 hour, in person, with them; change the date, time ("No time" beside it takes one off again) and hours (the time box, as a call's, takes the hour and minutes and starts on PM, so 7 30 is 7:30 pm: its AM/PM button, or typing a or p, changes that; the hour alone is on the hour, and an event being changed shows its own time), and add anyone else who was there by typing their name in the field under "With" (archived people too, after everyone else): who was there shows as chips beside "With", each with × to take them off; the "+ … others" box on that field's line takes how many more were there unnamed. Each is listed under "Hangouts" in Events (closed to start), the latest first ("2 hours · 4 Oct 2026, 7:30 pm", a trip "24 hours over 3 days · 4–6 Oct 2026, 6:00 pm to 2:00 pm"), with who else was there (a name opens their panel; "+20 others" after them for the unnamed) and its note, to change (Edit) or delete; it also sits in their chat at its time (at the start of its day without one; a trip where it started, saying until when), where a click opens the form to change or delete it. The Calendar shows a trip on each of its days, with that day's hours and which day of it that is (the month's sum counts it once); the Hangouts table in Events gives it one row, at its first day, with its hours in all; the Time river puts each day's hours in its month. Changing one opens the form on Hangout; Call on its toggle makes it a logged call (see "Logged calls"). Adding, changing or deleting one is a changeset, whose chip offers Undo straight away (as do `changes undo` and the map's Changes later), and marks everyone at it edited, so someone the rules archived is shown again. Hangouts are kept in the archive (`catch_up`, with its `time` and `others`, and a trip's `trip_last_day`, `trip_end_time`, `trip_private_from` and `trip_private_to`, all four null for one on a single day, and `catch_up_person` for who was at each; `modules/friend-map/server/catchUps.ts` is the only code that touches them), are yours alone (imports never write one, so a re-import leaves them as they are), follow a merge or join to the person kept, and go with a person deleted (a catch-up left with nobody goes too). No name except… hides their notes, as it hides people's. On the CLI, `catch-ups` lists them and `catch-up add`, `change` and `delete` make them (`--time 19:30` for when it started, `--others <n>` for the unnamed, `--until <day> --end <time>` for a trip, with `--private <from>-<to>` for its private hours and `--one-day` to make it one day again); SQL sees them, but never their notes, in the view `person_catch_up` (see queries.md's "SQL queries"); `npm run check-data` checks they are written as the app writes them, in person, with someone, never you.

**Logged calls.** A call the captain had that no import knows of (a call on another phone, say) is logged with Add event's **Call** (in their panel or chat, or the Calendar's day): its date (today or before), the time it started local time (needed: a call sits at its time), how long it lasted (whole minutes, `25`, or hours and minutes, `1:30`, up to a day), who was on it, one person or several (never you), as chips beside "With" as a hangout's are, added by typing a name, a short note if you like (one for the whole call, as its time and length are), and how many more were on it that you do not name, as a hangout's others (the CLI's `--others`, 0 unless said; the form does not ask yet, and a change from it keeps them). Each chip also says what the call was on with them, as its platform's badge with ▾: putting someone on opens, under the With line, their accounts that carry calls (phone, iMessage, shown as FaceTime, WhatsApp, Messenger, Instagram, Signal, Telegram, WeChat and Discord; not X), each as its platform's chip and its name, with the one their latest call was on already chosen (else their phone one, else the first), so one tap confirms it; ▾ on the chip opens them again to change it. Only someone with no such account has a call on no account, and their chip offers the apps instead (Phone to start, FaceTime, WhatsApp, Messenger, Instagram, Signal, Telegram, WeChat, Discord or Other; migration 51 in `server/archive/db.ts` added WhatsApp, Signal and Discord to what `manual_call.app` takes). A call with several people is kept as one logged call for each, on that person's account, all at one time and as long, sharing a call group; changing or deleting it from any one's chat (or deleting it in Events' Calls table, from its row's drawer) changes or deletes it for everyone on it, as one changeset. Each counts as a call imported on that account does (see "How closeness is scored"), except that a call with several people (its unnamed others among them) gives each of them their share of its time in the closeness score, as a hangout's worth is shared (an hour with three, at half shared, 40 minutes each), so it never counts as that many calls one to one, and that one logged on the phone or FaceTime with one person may be one the phone's call history brings in at its next import, so on a day with both, that person's calls logged so and their calls from the call history count the longer, never both (`callDays` in `modules/friend-map/server/calls.ts`; the call history has no calls with several people, so one logged with several adds up). It shows in their chat at its time ("Call · 7:30 pm · 25 min", its platform's chip, "with 2 others" when there were several, "you logged it", its note's first line, a click opening the form on Call with everyone on it, with Delete), under its account in Accounts (a phone account's calls, or another account's calls after its messages), in the calls added up under Events, in the Calendar (one call however many were on it, listed once with everyone on their platform), and in the closeness score with the day's other calls (and the Events chart, whose calls for a month are their share). Each goes with its account: a merge, a join or "Not them" takes it wherever the account goes, as an imported call. Adding, changing or deleting one is a changeset with the chip's Undo, and marks everyone on it edited. **Add call** in the Calls table on the Events page's Tables logs one too, from its When (a date and a time), Length, With, what it was **On** (Their usual, each on the account they are usually called on, as a new chip starts; or an app, each on their account there, someone with no account that carries calls on the app itself, and someone whose accounts are elsewhere refused, naming theirs) and Note; **Delete this call** at the foot of a logged call's drawer deletes it for everyone on it. Both are edits in the tables' record as well as changesets. An imported call is never deleted: its drawer says so. **A call or a hangout, either way.** A logged call and a hangout are both events, so the form changing one offers the other kind on its toggle, as Add event does: a call made a hangout keeps its date, time, everyone on it and its note, its length becoming hours (to two places, as a hangout keeps them: 25 minutes is 0.42 hours), in person; a hangout made a call keeps its date, its time (one without asks for it, as a call needs one), everyone named at it, each on their usual account as someone put on a new call is, and its note, its hours becoming whole minutes (at least 1), and its unnamed others, which the call keeps (the form, which does not ask for them, still says they go). Save as call or Save as hangout deletes the one and adds the other in its place as one changeset (`POST /api/archive/catch-ups/:id/call`, `POST /api/archive/calls/:id/catch-up`; `People.catchUpToCall` and `callToCatchUp`), whose chip says "Made it a …" with Undo, as the map's Changes and `changes undo` do later; Delete is not offered while the toggle is on the other kind. Only calls you logged can be: an imported call (from a platform's records or the phone's call history) never opens the form, so it stays a call. They are kept in the archive (`manual_call`: one row for each person on it, with the call group, the account, when it started, how long, its note and its unnamed others; on no account, the person and the app instead; someone a merge leaves on one twice counts once, and the next change of it keeps one; `modules/friend-map/server/manualCalls.ts` is the only code that writes them, and `modules/friend-map/server/calls.ts` reads them with the imported calls, `PERSON_CALLS`), are yours alone (imports never write one, so a re-import neither deletes nor repeats one), those on no account follow a merge or join, and those go with a person deleted. On the CLI, `calls` lists them among a person's calls with their number, and `call add <person> [person ...]`, `change` (`--people` for everyone on it, `--note` for its note, `--note ""` for none, `--others <n>` for those unnamed) and `delete` make them (`--account <account>`, once for each person you choose for, the account saying whose; `--app` for someone with no account that carries calls), for everyone on it; SQL sees them in `person_call` (`manual_call_id`, `call_group`, `people`, how many were on it, and `others`, those unnamed); `npm run check-data` checks them, their notes written as the app writes notes, and that a group's calls share one time, length, note and unnamed others. An imported call keeps its own behaviour: a call record whose app says no length is given one with "Add length" in the chat or in Events' Calls table, or is said to be missed with the chat's "No answer" (`call_answered`, as Answered: No in Events' Calls table, which also says Yes or Not known over what its source says; the chat reads it back as `callAnswer`, and Remove takes it away).

**Hangouts by call.** Before calls were their own kind a hangout could be ticked **by call** (`catch_up.by_call`). On 29 October 2026 each became the call it was (`repair by-call-catch-ups`, since removed), and migration 61 in `server/archive/db.ts` dropped the column, so no hangout can be a call: it refuses to run while any catch-up is still by call, naming the release before it (commit 499570f), whose `npm run archive -- repair by-call-catch-ups --apply` makes each the call it was. The change log keeps what it logged: a changeset from before still shows "by call", and undoing one that would bring a hangout back by call is refused (its call is the call it was); the rest undo as before. friend-map's contract dropped `byCall` in 1.2.0: the archive sends none, passes over `false` from an older page, and still refuses `true`.

**Notes on calls.** Any call can carry a short note of yours, as a hangout does: what you talked about. Set it in Events' Calls table (its Note, in a row's drawer), or for a call you logged in Add event's form too; it shows under the call in their chat (its first line; No name except… hides it, as it hides hangouts' notes). A logged call keeps it on its rows (`manual_call.note`); an imported call keeps it apart, so imports never touch it (`call_note`, `modules/friend-map/server/calls.ts`'s `setCallNote`): one row for each call you have given a note, a call record in a chat by its message, a call in no chat by its source's own key (its platform and call id, which every row of a call with several people shares and a re-import keeps); a note taken away leaves its row, emptied, so undo always finds it. Each change is a changeset, with the chip's Undo; it touches nobody (see "Untouched"), and counts in nothing. Notes are content, as hangouts' are: SQL never reads them (`person_call` has none), and the CLI's `call add` and `change` take `--note` for a logged call's; `npm run check-data` checks that each is written as the app writes notes and is on a call still in the archive.

**Identicons.** Everyone has an identicon, a small picture as crypto wallets give an address: eight cells square, the left half drawn at random and mirrored onto the right, each cell a dark, soft background, a light main colour or a mid spot colour of another hue (`src/identicon.ts`, which the server and the page share; `src/Identicon.tsx` draws it). Only its seed is stored (`person.identicon_seed`, 16 hex digits), so the drawing can be retuned without touching anyone. A person is given theirs when they are made (by an import, by hand, as you, or by "Not them"), a hash of their id and when they were made, so the same archive always gives the same pictures; a merge or a join keeps the kept person's; the rules give one to any row made without one, and `npm run check-data` names anyone without. Everyone there was got theirs in migration 20 in `server/archive/db.ts`, by the same rule. The colours are picked in OKLCH, so each role's lightness is what the eye sees whatever the hue: the main colour stands out from the background, the spot from both, the main colour reads on the map's night sky (and tints the person's name there), and the dark background shows against the light panel (tests check these over thousands of seeds). It shows in the person panel beside the name, in People's list and in Orbits' hover card, and every Orbits marker is filled with it. Clicking it in the person panel opens a picker (`src/IconPicker.tsx`, its draft in `src/iconDraft.ts`) for drafting a new one (the one they have stays in view in the panel): colour and picture change apart, so twelve colours and a hue slider for any colour between them recolour the draft and keep its picture, **New picture** gives a new picture at random in the draft's colour, and **New colour** keeps the picture and moves its colour at random at least 60° round the wheel. Nothing is saved while drafting: **Save** saves the draft as one change, picture and colour together, and closes the picker; ×, Esc or a click elsewhere closes it without saving. The colour is kept as a hue beside the seed (`person.identicon_hue`, whole degrees 0 to 359, added in migration 40; null for the seed's own colour), so their main colour is that hue at a fixed lightness and chroma, the swatch's own, and the background and spot go round with it; it is their colour everywhere, Orbits' marker, name and glow among them. Each Save is one changeset (`People.setIdenticon`, through `PUT /api/archive/people/:id/identicon` with the drafted seed and hue), with the chip's Undo ("New colour for …", or "New icon for …" when the colour stayed) and in the change log, and marks them edited. A read-only page has no picker.

**Untouched.** Someone you have never changed by hand is untouched: no name, alias or tag of yours (archiving or bringing them back by hand included), no location, note or reaching out, no hangout or logged call with them, no merge, "Join person" or confirmed proposal, no account you linked to them, and no "Not them" taking an account from them (the fresh person "Not them" makes is touched too, its account linked by you). Imports, the rules (joins by name, archive rules) and notes from the address book touch nobody, and undoing a change puts back whether they were untouched. It is decided in one place, `People.untouched` in `modules/friend-map/server/people.ts`, from what is stored (`person.edited_at`, the accounts linked by you, and the pairs "Not them" remembers), and sent as `untouched` on each person; you never are. The people views draw them muted, so they stand apart from those you have looked after: grey in a group chat's members (green for the rest), a grey name in People's list, saying why on hover (`src/untouched.ts`); Orbits and the map do not. A group chat's "Archive untouched" archives them (see archive.md's "Who is archived").

### Moving the person files in

The first time this code opens an archive from before the person table (schema version 4 or older), it moves the person files in, in one transaction (migration 5 in `modules/friend-map/server/db.ts`): the `account` table becomes `import_person`, the person table is rebuilt, and every file in `data/friends/` and `data/people/`, read the way the app used to read them, keeps its person (its id), with the file's name, aliases and tags, marked as edited by you, and the accounts it listed linked to it by you. A file tagged `me` gives its accounts to you. "Not them" and rejected proposals are kept, a decision about a file now being about its person. Everything else is made again by the rules, which run straight after (joins by name, archive rules, proposals, counts). A file that can't be read is named on the server's output and left out. The files are left on disk exactly as they were, and are never read again; what only they held (a friendship `identity`, a primary contact platform, dated notes, other frontmatter keys) is not moved and stays in them. The server prints "Running the rules…" while this happens, which takes a few seconds on a big archive.

## Changing data from a chat

Friend data is changed from a chat with an agent opened in this folder (Claude Code or Codex), not from the Query bar's box, whose Generate only writes a query. An instruction to change people ("for this group, tag everyone as commercial who I have not tagged myself") may need a question back, and a mistake needs undoing, which a chat does better than one round trip; and it keeps that work out of the desktop agent's context. The agent works only through `npm run archive`, and everything it changes is in the change log, where the captain sees it and can undo it. AGENTS.md tells it how.

**The change log.** Every edit to people, from the app (its routes: the person panel, proposals, "Join person", "Not them"; and Events' People and Tags tables, through the core's edit route, `modules/friend-map/server/peopleEdits.ts`, each named as the panel's routes name it) or from the CLI, is one changeset in friend map's archive (the `changeset` table; the core's change log, `server/archive/changes.ts`, over the module's tables, `modules/friend-map/server/changeLog.ts`; the inspirations' changesets are the inspirations module's own, in `modules/inspirations/inspirations.sqlite`): when, from where (`app` or `cli`), what was done, the captain's words when an agent passed them (`--why`), the backup the CLI took just before, and every row it changed in the people tables (the person, their tags and location, the tag and value lists, which person each account is, and pairs said to be different), or a group chat dropped or brought back, or someone taken out of one or put back, or a hangout (catch-up) and who was at it, or a call you logged (`manual_call`), or the length you gave a call (`call_length`), or an inspiration's caption, tags or hidden and the inspiration tags (see features.md's "Inspirations"), as it was before and after (`change_row`); and drafts a merge moved or a delete took (`draft`), which never refuse an undo, since a draft is saved outside any changeset. The rows are written by triggers only while a changeset is being recorded, so the rules, imports and live sync, which write the same tables, are never logged. An edit that changes nothing leaves no changeset. From the rows the log shows each person's before and after for what changed (`tags: friend -> friend, commercial`; `hangout: (none) -> 2 hours, 4 Oct 2026`; `logged call: (none) -> 25 min on FaceTime, 4 Oct 2026, 7:30 pm`; `call on 3 Mar 2026: (none) -> 25 min`, on the person the call was with) and what else changed (a tag or value added, renamed or deleted, the tag order, two people said to be different, a group chat dropped or brought back, someone taken out of a group chat or put back). Changesets are kept for 90 days; older ones are deleted when the archive opens.

**Undo** puts back exactly what one changeset changed, as a changeset of its own (so undoing the undo does it again). It is refused, naming the people, when anything the changeset set has been changed again since (the tag it gave someone taken off them, the location it set changed), or when putting it back would change what was done since (deleting a tag it made that someone else has been given since, or a person it added who has been given an account). Anything else changed since is kept, a person's other fields too: undoing a name change leaves the location set since. What is worked out from people (who the rules archive, the counts, proposals) is worked out again for those it touched, and when someone was last edited goes back only if nothing has changed it since. A changeset once undone can't be undone again; undo its undo instead.

**Every CLI change takes a checked backup first**, as `backup` does, and ends by naming its changeset: `Changeset #12, after the backup 2026-09-30T140300-before-edit-tag-commercial.sqlite; undo it with: npm run archive -- changes undo 12`. The backups are pruned as any others (see data.md's "Backups"), so only the one before the day's latest change stays, for 48 hours; undo is the way back from anything earlier.

**Bulk edits.** `edit` makes one edit to many people, chosen with the Query language over everyone, archived people too (see queries.md's "The query language"; `group:` finds the people in a group chat), or with SQL (see queries.md's "SQL queries": `--query "SELECT person_id FROM people WHERE ..."`), or by number with `--people 12,34`. Without `--apply` it is a dry run: who it would change, each before and after, who is that way already, and any tag or value it would add; nothing is written. `--apply` makes it to the same choice, chosen again inside the changeset, and only if it would change exactly `--expect` people (the dry run's count), so what the captain was shown is what is done:

```sh
npm run archive -- groups footy                 # #75  footy (messenger, 17 people, last 2026-08-30)
npm run archive -- edit tag commercial --query 'group:#75 AND untagged'
npm run archive -- edit tag commercial --query 'group:#75 AND untagged' --apply --expect 16 --why "for the footy group, tag everyone as commercial who I have not tagged myself"
npm run archive -- changes                      # the log, newest first, with who each touched
npm run archive -- changes show 12              # everyone it touched, before and after, and its backup
npm run archive -- changes undo 12              # refused, naming who, if any of it has changed since
```

The edits are `tag` and `untag` (one tag or several; `archive` hides someone, off the map and every view, and `keep` keeps them out of the archive rules), `location` and `clear-location`, `reach-out` and `no-reach-out`, and, for one person at a time, `name` and `aliases` (each alias one word or quoted) or `clear-aliases`. Merges and "Not them" stay on `merge` and `detach`; every other command that changes people (`person ...`, `tag ...`, `field ...`, `confirm`, `reject`, `contact-notes`), and `group drop`, `group restore`, `group remove-member`, `group put-back` and `catch-up add`, `change` and `delete`, is a changeset too, and takes `--why`. `groups --person <person>` lists the group chats someone is in, untitled ones named by the first few people in them. One instruction that takes several edits ("tag them commercial and hide them") stays one changeset, undone as one: each edit after the first adds to it with `--into <changeset>`, which takes only the newest changeset, from the CLI, neither an undo nor undone.

**In the app**, "Changes" in the header of Friend Map's live map views (the Table's, until it folded into Events' Tables on 10 November 2026; not on a phone) lists the recent changesets, newest first: what was done, when, from the app or the CLI, the captain's words, and who it touched (a name opens their panel), with Details for each person's before and after, and Undo, after a confirm; an undo refused says who has changed since. It follows changes made anywhere, as every view does (`GET /api/archive/changes`, `/changes/:id`, `POST /changes/:id/undo`; `src/demos/ChangesPanel.tsx`).
