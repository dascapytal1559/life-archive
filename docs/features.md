# The modules and the core's pages

What each module and each of the core's pages does. docs/modules.md has how a module is built.

## Modules

The archive is a core and modules, by the captain's word ("you just have a singular server that takes in stuff and then all of these modules become like plugins to the core"), so that its parts can be shared one by one: "they can pick rituals as a module, exercise as a module, friend map as a module". A module is a folder `modules/<id>/` with two entry files the core loads, `module.ts` (its server half: its route at `/life-archive/api/m/<id>`, the services it offers, its made-up data, its privacy lines) and, when it has a page, `viewer.tsx` (its page half: its aspects and views in the console, a view of which may be a tab on another page, Events' say); it imports the core through the SDK alone (`sdk/`), and the core finds it by its place (`server/modules/loader.ts`, `viewer/modules.ts`), never by name. `settings.json`'s `modules` switches one off (`{"health": false}`). Health is the first module (`modules/health/`, see "Health"), exercise the second (`modules/exercise/`, see "Exercise"), the first with a database of its own (`modules/<id>/<file>` in the archive's folder, which the core opens for it and the NAS backup sends as a checked copy), connectors the core's host runs, rows of its own in the calendar, a table on the Events page and a command (`npm run exercise`), journal the third (`modules/journal/`, see "Journal"), the first with a job outside the server, the launchd agent its command installs (`npm run journal`), and with no page half until its Journal page came on 9 November 2026, and rituals the fourth (`modules/rituals/`, see "Rituals"), rituals' own code brought in from its repository, whose store is its own code's (`modules/rituals/data/ritual.sqlite3`, which `bin/ritualctl` writes and the module reads in-process), its rows in the calendar kept in step by a watch while the server runs, a table and a tab on the Events page, a service the exercise module asks for and a command (`npm run rituals`), and friend map the fifth (`modules/friend-map/`, its README; see people.md's "People" and archive.md's "The message archive"), the social layer: the message archive (its database, `modules/friend-map/archive.sqlite`), the twelve chat connectors and the jobs that bring this Mac's and the Cua Space's exports, people and their rules, friend-map's contract at `/api/archive/` and its page at `/friend-map/` (paths outside `/life-archive/api/m/` it mounts, and a page the core serves for it), its rows in the calendar, the Messages, Calls, Hangouts and People tables, the Friend Map aspect and the people commands, which `npm run archive` hands to it; and shopping the sixth (`modules/shopping/`, see "Shopping"), a page of the core's until it moved, its route still at the address it had (a mount) and its morning pulls work of its own while the server runs; and the inspirations (`modules/inspirations/`, see "Inspirations"), a page of the core's until it moved too, its routes at the address they had, its database and kept files its own, taken over once from the core's; each gone whole when it is switched off (friend map's switch takes `/api/archive/` and `/friend-map/` with it); the rest of the console is the core's until the plan's next steps move it. Every folder under `modules/` is ignored by git but the ones named in `.gitignore`, so a private module cloned in is never committed, and neither its privacy lines nor anything else of it reach a tracked file (`docs/privacy.md` holds the modules git tracks). A module has no packages of its own: its `package.json` is its manifest, and the packages it needs are the root's (rituals' two Effect packages, `@effect/platform-node` and `@effect/sql-sqlite-node`, are in the root `package.json` since it came). `docs/modules.md` has the contract, the loaders, the switches and how a module is tested.

## Health

The Health tab (the console's Health Record, `/life-archive/dev/health-record`; the health module, `modules/health/`, see "Modules", on this Mac and on the tailnet) shows the owner's health results: every blood test, screen, body scan and physical assessment the archive keeps, each judged by one authority on range, graded by how far past its line, and ordered so what needs looking at is at the top. There is no summary: out first (far out at its top), then mildly out, at the edge (still in range), in range, then what is under treatment (a result a treatment answers), then what nothing judges. Its own row of tabs slices that: All (everything but the sample's quality, the default), Physical (the body scan, blood pressure, and the highest heart rate recorded and the physical assessment, which on this tab come first, above the results), Cholesterol (the lipids), Allergies (the specific IgE results), Records (what is no lab result: the vaccines given, the medicines prescribed and dispensed, and Medicare's services and PBS medicines, each a table newest first; see "Records" below) and Sources (what is rarely read: the sample-quality results, the Haemolysis index, which leave All and its counts, and the page's account of how results are judged, the star key, every source and when the health exports were last read; the other tabs end with a one-line pointer to it and to Records, and the "Not read yet" warning stays on every tab), each ordered and counted the same way and kept in the address (`/life-archive/dev/health-record/cholesterol`); which tests a tab holds is set in one place, `modules/health/viewer/tabs.ts`, by body system and, where a system is wider than the tab, by test key. Within a status, the further outside (or the nearer the edge) first, then by name. **Group by** (a switch under the counts, `modules/health/viewer/grouping.ts`) groups the results by status, as above (the default), or by category: one section per body system, in the catalogue's order (`SYSTEMS` in `modules/health/server/catalogue.ts`, sent as the answer's `systems`), each listing its results worst first by the same order, a system the catalogue does not list (Other) last. The choice is kept in the address's query (`?group=category`; status is none), beside the tab in its path, and kept from tab to tab. Each system has its own colour (`--cat-<key>` in `viewer/viewer.css`), only ever a dot and a tinted outline, never a fill, so none reads as a status: by status, each test's system is a chip at the end of its date line, in its colour; by category, the section's heading is in its colour and the cards leave the chip out, the heading saying it. By category, each section's heading (a button, with a chevron) folds it and opens it again: a folded one keeps its dot, its count and how many of it are out ("2 out", in red) or else mildly out (in yellow). Every section starts open; which are folded is remembered on this device only (localStorage, `life-archive.health.collapsed`), not in the address, and the page folds the same without it. By status the sections do not fold. Every number is a chart, a test done once too (one dot): value up the side, date along the bottom, each dot solid in its status colour, the latest result on each chart a step bigger (radius 6 pixels) than every other (4.5; `modules/health/viewer/dots.ts`). Where several results are joined by the dashed line, each step carries an arrowhead in the line's colour pointing forward in time, centred in the part of the step the two dots leave showing so no dot hides it, and left out where the dots nearly touch (`arrowHeads`). Every chart on every tab over the same days (from the first of January of the year of the earliest result of any kind to today, where the axis ends, marked by a line with no label) and at the same width, so a date sits at the same place down the page. The page shows three colours, on its chips, dots and counts and the bands behind each chart: green in range (at the edge too), yellow mildly out, red out, which takes in far out (the server still grades far out; `modules/health/viewer/format.ts` folds it). Behind the results, the range's rungs as those three bands, and only where an authority sets them: a range with one limit ("3.0 or under") is in range all the way on the side it does not bound, so green runs from the line to the chart's edge there, with no yellow or red beyond it. The scale is fixed for the test's results and draws only the bands they reach (`scaleOf` in `modules/health/server/judge.ts`): on each side of the range, where no result goes past the limit, the chart ends at the limit itself, with no yellow or red; where results do, it runs out to the end of the band the farthest of them falls in, and no further. Only yellow and red are trimmed this way; the green is always drawn: a two-sided range's green runs to its other limit, and a side no limit bounds with no result on it is drawn as tall as the out side drawn opposite, so the limit sits mid-chart (a made-up result of 4.1 against an upper limit of 3.0, mildly out: green 1.86 to 3.0, yellow 3.0 to 4.14). A band with no end, and a side of the range no limit bounds, ends where the farthest result sits mid-way between the band's start (or the limit) and the edge (limit 7.0, result 8.9: the edge at 10.8; `openEdge`), and a quantity whose range is above 0 is never drawn below 0. Where an age band changes, the widest over the bands, a band with no result adding only its limits. A limit the chart ends at carries no label of its own: the side shows only the ordinary ticks. The scale moves only when a new result arrives. Never stretched to fit a result: a result past it sits hollow on the edge. A screen keeps its words, and its chip says them by the test's own data (the catalogue's `words`): Clear (green) for the expected negative, Immune (green) for a protective antibody found (hepatitis B surface antibody), Positive (red) for anything else it reads, counted and ordered as in range and out. Its line is a plain one ("Lab screen: negative is the expected result"), and its chart, on the same days as every other, is a green band with each result a dot in its middle labelled with its word, a red band above it with the positive's dot only when a result is positive, and no value axis (`ScreenChart`). Under each, what it was judged by and its source, and who graded it past the line, and a toggle named by the places that reported it, in the order they first did (each lab's name as the data gives it, up to any comma), that slides open on each result as printed: the lab, the printed name, the lab's own range, how else it was printed (`34 mmol/mol`, `class II`), and the file and page it came from.

**One authority on range** (`modules/health/server/ranges.ts`, `npm run health -- ranges` to read it). Every test has one entry: its source and its bands by age and sex, or why it has none. A result is judged by the band for the person's age on the day the sample was collected, worked out from the date of birth; so the interval moves only where an age band ends (urea's at 50 and 70, say) or when the table is changed for a new guideline, which changes its version. The lab's printed range is kept as evidence and never colours or orders anything. The primary source is the RCPA's harmonised adult reference intervals (the AACB's, endorsed by the RCPA, as published in Koerbin et al. 2019); a test they do not cover names one source of its own: the AACB's harmonised lipid decision limits (2018), KDIGO for eGFR, WHO and the Australian HbA1c statement for blood sugar, the ANZ vitamin D statement, the EAS Lp(a) consensus, the NHMRC on lead, the Immunisation Handbook for hepatitis B immunity, ImmunoCAP's current cut-off (0.1 kU/L) for allergy results, the Heart Foundation for blood pressure, WHO for BMI, the ISCD for bone density Z-scores (for men under 50; a man's from 50 is read by T-score, which the archive does not keep, and a woman's turns on menopause, so neither is judged) and EWGSOP2 for lean mass. Where that source sets no limit on one side and another trusted source does, that side's line is the other's (`lines` in `ranges.ts`, `lineSource`), with its own stars, account and links, and a result past it is out by it unless it grades further: vitamin D's upper limit, 125 nmol/L, by the IOM (2011, two stars); fasting glucose's lower limit, 3.0 mmol/L, by the Endocrine Society (2009, two stars), graded under it by CTCAE (grade 2 out, grades 3 and 4 far out, from 2.2); and HDL's upper limit, 2.3 mmol/L, by the ESC/EAS 2019 guideline (two stars) as two sources quote it, since its own text could not be opened, which its account, citation and name ("as quoted") say, linking the guideline and the article that quotes it. A test none of those covers (`LAB_TESTS` in `ranges.ts`) is judged by a lab's printed interval, which the owner chooses once and keeps whichever lab later runs the test, in `modules/health/lab-intervals.json` in the archive's folder, never in code, since which labs tested someone and when is their own record (`modules/health/server/labIntervals.ts`: each lab's key, name, the year and reports it printed them on, whether it was the last to run them, the sex they were printed for, and each test's interval); without it those tests are not judged, and say where the interval goes; a lab's lower limit of 0 on a count (basophils, say) is no limit, so those are upper limits alone. A test with no defensible authority (albumin, whose methods differ too much for one interval; a glucose taken without fasting; body fat) says why and is never coloured. "At the edge" is within 5% of the interval's width, or of its one limit, from a limit.

**Grades past the line, and stars.** The line never moves; past it a result is graded, in up to three grades (mildly out, out, far out; fewer where the source has fewer, never made up), each rung naming its source, in a fixed order. First the line's own source, where it publishes grades (the Heart Foundation's high-normal and grades of hypertension, WHO's BMI classes, WHO's impaired fasting glucose and diabetes, the ADS's higher-risk HbA1c and diabetes, the vitamin D statement's mild, moderate and severe deficiency, KDIGO's G3a to G5, the EAS's Lp(a) grey zone, rule-in and very high). Else a second named source, from the line out: NCEP ATP III for LDL and total cholesterol, the National Lipid Association for non-HDL, the Endocrine Society for triglycerides (mg/dL converted at 38.67 for cholesterol, the stated mmol/L for triglycerides), and NCI CTCAE v5.0 for the salts, liver enzymes, creatinine, bilirubin and blood counts it grades (grade 1 mildly out, 2 out, 3 and 4 far out, its limit of normal the line). Else nothing grades it: past the line is out, by the line's own source, with no mildly out, as no authority backs a finer grade. The dashboard's own rule (mildly out to 10% of the interval's width, or of its one limit, past the line, out to 25%, far out beyond) is kept in `ranges.ts` as `PERCENT`, off behind `PERCENT_RULE = false`, since nothing without an authority is shown. Every source has stars by one rubric (`STARS` in `ranges.ts`), each with a reason: three, a national or international clinical standard made for exactly this use and population; two, a respected authority used outside its exact purpose, population or era, or with coarse grades (CTCAE, NCEP, the NLA, the Endocrine Society, ImmunoCAP, ISCD, EWGSOP2, the EAS); one, a single lab's printed interval; none, the dashboard's own rule (off). Each grade names its rung's source (`judge.ts`), and the modal shows each band's stars: LDL of 3.4 is mildly out, past the AACB's line (three stars), graded by NCEP (two).

Each result is one row of a 12-column grid: its description (name, grade and stars, latest value, what it was judged by, as printed) in 6 columns and its chart in the other 6; on a phone the two stack. A chart is drawn at the width it is given, not scaled, so its labels keep their size; where there is no room it labels fewer years and points, always the latest.

**How each range was built.** Each result's grade (its chip, with no stars on it) opens a modal telling how that range and its grades were built. First the range the latest result was judged by as the page's three bands, from out above to out below (a red band listing each grade it takes in), each with its bounds, its source's own name for it, its source and that source's stars, the result's own rung marked (in range naming the source of each limit when they differ). An out row that only restates the far side of the in-range row (the one band past a line, from the line's own source, with no bound or grade of its own, as transferrin's "over 3.2") is left out, the in-range row saying it all (`restatesLine`); one from another source (vitamin D's upper limit) or graded past the line (LDL) stays. Then each source's stars and why. Then how the line was built from its authority: what the source publishes, what was taken from it and how (bounds, units, any conversion), whether it is a population reference interval, a decision limit, a lab's printed interval chosen once or a result read by its words, for a side whose line another source sets, that source's own account and stars, every band the table holds for the test with the one that applied to the latest result (the band for the sex and the age that day), what the source does not cover and what the page does instead, how each grading source's grades were taken (units, conversions) or that no authority grades a side, so past it is out, nothing finer, then each source's citation and a link to the official source where one is kept, and to any source it was read through. The accounts are data in `modules/health/server/rangeAccounts.ts`, next to `ranges.ts`: one per source, with what is particular to a test once per test, each saying where its wording came from; a new test judged by a source gets its account by itself, and a test fails if a source has none. A test with no authority opens the reason it is not judged.

**Where the results come from.** Two platforms' exports are read, each in `platform-exports/<platform>/<export date>/` as every platform's are: Everlab's (`everlab/`, one folder per collection date and lab inside) and My Health Record's (`my-health-record/`: the labs' own reports it holds, and its immunisation, medicines and Medicare pages, printed from myGov). `npm run health -- import` reads every PDF in both, each by the lab report it is: an Everlab "Pathology Results Report" (one panel per PDF, whichever lab ran it) by `modules/health/server/everlab.ts`, and QML Pathology's own report (as My Health Record attaches it) by `modules/health/server/qml.ts`, each from its text as lines of cells that PDFKit gives through `modules/health/server/pdfLines.swift` (macOS's own PDF reader, run with `swift`; there is no `pdftotext` here), each printed name matched to its test by `modules/health/server/catalogue.ts`. The QML reader takes the rows indented under a section's title, whatever order PDFKit gives the page's columns in, and reads nothing laid out otherwise (two results run together on one line, or a name and its result in one cell); a report none of whose results it reads is listed unread. The PDFs in another format (a lab's report the readers do not know, the DEXA scan, the physical assessment, a web page printed from a portal) are transcribed by hand into `modules/health/by-hand.json`, each result with its file and page, and the import leaves those files to it, as it does the files by-hand.json lists as holding nothing to import (`skipped`: a viewer's page of a report beside the report itself, an earlier print of the same page). It writes `modules/health/imported.json` whole, so it can be run again at any time, and says what each file gave (never the results), a PDF it could not read (to transcribe), and a printed name no test has (to add to `catalogue.ts`; until then it shows as a test nothing judges). A panel exported twice is kept once. `--dry-run` writes nothing.

**One result, many copies.** Everlab's export and My Health Record's hold many of the same lab reports. One lab's result for one test on one day is shown once (`sameResultOnce` in `modules/health/server/store.ts`), whichever files print it: Everlab's copy leads (the order of `HEALTH_EXPORTS`), and the other copies' files join its places, so the toggle under the result lists both. A copy that prints the result differently is kept beside it as "also printed as", and the import names each such result (`PRINTED DIFFERENTLY`, the files only, never the values) for someone to look at.

```sh
npm run health -- import --dry-run   # what each file gives
npm run health -- import             # writes modules/health/imported.json (health/imported.json while an archive's records are still in health/; data.md's "Backups")
npm run health -- ranges             # the authority: each test, its bands and source
```

`modules/health/by-hand.json` (reviewed by hand, never written by code) also holds whose results they are (`person`: date of birth and sex, which every band is chosen by), the physical assessment as the report has it, runs transcribed by hand from Polar Flow's share pages (`runs`: each marked trusted or not by hand, a chest strap's, such as a Polar H10, trusted; a watch's heart rate during a run is never trusted; see "Exercise"), treatments that answer a system's results (`treatments`: the results they answer then sit under treatment, never at the top), and the records that are no lab result (see "Records"). The server reads both files on each request, so an edit shows at once.

**Records.** The Records tab shows what My Health Record and Medicare keep that is no lab result, from by-hand.json, each record with the files (and pages) it was read from: `immunisations` (the day given, what against and the vaccine, from the immunisation register's statement and My Health Record's page), `medicines` (each prescription and dispense: the day, prescribed or dispensed, the medicine, brand, directions and supply), and `medicare`'s `services` (each MBS item: the day, item number, description and provider, and, where a claims statement has it, the fee, Medicare's benefit, the gap and how it was paid) and `pbs` (each medicine supplied under the PBS: prescribed and supplied days, medicine, brand, form, quantity, repeats and code). The portals print these as web pages whose text PDFKit gives out of order, so they are transcribed rather than read by code. `transcriptions` names the files that are someone's transcription of a portal rather than its own print (a portal page that prints five rows a page, copied out whole by an agent, say): a record read only from those carries a Transcribed tag on the page, and its section says which file and why; a record also found in a platform's own print (the claims statement, the overview's first page, the dispense records) lists that file too and carries no tag.

**What the immunisations protect against.** Above the doses, the Records tab shows one row per disease the immunisations name, from one lookup in code (`modules/health/server/immunisations.ts`), never by changing by-hand.json. A printed "against" is split into its diseases by the longest names the lookup knows ("Diphtheria Tetanus Pertussis" is three), and a print naming none it knows is read by the vaccine's brand. Each disease shows its everyday name beside the medical one where they differ ("Pertussis (whooping cough)"), in the summary and in the dose table. Each row gives the latest dose, how many days' doses name it, and how long protection runs from that dose, as the Australian Immunisation Handbook sets it for adults (each disease cites its Handbook page; read on 2026-10-20): until a booster interval ends (10 years for diphtheria, tetanus, pertussis and polio, 3 for typhoid, 1 for influenza and COVID-19, 6 months for COVID-19 from 75), lasting (lifelong for yellow fever; no booster set after a complete course of hepatitis A or B, measles, mumps, rubella, varicella or zoster, or Q fever's one dose), or no interval where the Handbook sets none for adults (pneumococcal, meningococcal, rabies and others). A course the record holds fewer doses of than the Handbook's (hepatitis A's two, hepatitis B's three, counted by distinct days) shows as incomplete until its next dose is due. A disease or vaccine the lookup does not know keeps its row, its interval blank rather than guessed. The page draws each interval as a bar from the dose to its end with today marked, the part left in blue, an ended one grey (never red), with the Handbook's words for adults and a link to its page, under one line saying this is general information, not medical advice (`modules/health/viewer/Protection.tsx`). A course is counted by disease, not brand, so a combined hepatitis A and B vaccine's three-dose course shows hepatitis A complete after two.

**Heart rate and the physical assessment.** The physical assessment shows each number by where it came from: measured at the visit (blood pressure, also judged with the other results), Everlab's test output (VO₂ max and the zone heart rates; the report names its calculators but shows neither method nor inputs), from a wearable as of the day it was printed (not the visit), and Everlab's own scores; then what is not in it: the maximum heart rate, and any part of the assessment the printout lacks. The highest heart rate a trusted recording holds is shown as measured, with its device and day, and as what it is: a floor for the true maximum, not the maximum itself; beside it, how far Everlab's zone 5 sits below it. An age formula appears only labelled as an estimate. The workouts themselves are on Fitness (see "Exercise"): a line here links to it, and the peak's day opens the activity it was recorded in there.

**The highest heart rate** is taken from the Fitness page's activities (the exercise module's `Exercise` service, which health asks for optionally, so with that module off the tab shows none: `modules/exercise/server/activities.ts`'s `peakOf`, see "Exercise"): the highest maximum among those whose heart rate is trusted, the earliest of equals, naming its sensor, what recorded it, its source and the activity it was recorded in. The runs themselves, and the count of workouts whose heart rate is not trusted, are on Exercise, every workout shown there, trusted or not.

**On the tailnet, with the rest of the viewer.** Health results are content. The page asks `GET /life-archive/api/m/health` (`modules/health/server/http.ts`), under `/life-archive/`, so Tailscale Serve passes it on and the owner's phone has the tab as it has every other; the tailnet's ACL is what keeps it to their own devices. The route refuses a Host the door does not admit, a request from another site's page, and anything but a read. Real results stay in the archive's folder; the tests and the testing data use a made-up man's (`modules/health/fixtures/health.ts`, written as real PDFs by `modules/health/fixtures/makePdf.swift` so the whole path, PDFKit included, is tested), and `npm run testing-data` puts them in every testing folder.

## Exercise

The console's Fitness (`/life-archive/dev/fitness`, on this Mac and on the tailnet; Exercise until it was renamed, and its old `/life-archive/dev/exercise` still opens it; the module keeps its id, `exercise`) shows on its Activities tab every workout the archive has, the ones logged in Rituals with the rest, laid out as the training logs people know do it (Strava's, say), since that is an interface people are used to, in the console's own look rather than any app's branding or colours.

**It is a module** (`modules/exercise/`, its README, and "Modules"; the second after health, and the first with a store of its own, the proof of the plan's shape): its database, `modules/exercise/exercise.sqlite` in the archive's folder (its index of workouts, its tombstones and its two connectors' runs and cursors), which the core opens before the archive and keeps for it; its connectors, `polar` and `polar-export` (see events.md's "Workouts from Polar"), run by the core's host, each import applied to the module's database and what it changed pushed into the calendar as the host would push its own; its route, under `/life-archive/api/m/exercise/`; the `Exercise` service health asks for; its rows' answers for the calendar's drawers and its every row for a rebuild; the Workouts table on the Events page; its command, `npm run exercise -- status | polar login | map download | map list` (`npm run map` is the last two); its made-up workouts for the testing data; and its lines of the privacy policy (`modules/exercise/server/privacy.ts`). `"modules": {"exercise": false}` in the archive's `settings.json` switches it off whole, the page, the tab, the connectors and the maps with it, and the rest of the archive is as it was (the Health tab shows no highest heart rate). It asks the core optionally for what is not its own: the runs transcribed by hand (health's `RunsByHand`) and the workouts logged in Rituals (the rituals module's `RitualWorkouts`, read from rituals' store). When it first ran beside an archive that held its rows, it copied them in once, ids kept (the take-over, `modules/exercise/server/db.ts`: the workout events with their detail, the tombstones, and the polar connectors' runs and cursors), so every activity kept its address and the live connector went on from where it was; the archive's rows from before stay until `npm run archive -- migrate friend-map-module --apply` (the friend map step's move) overwrites them for good, which is refused until the module's database has taken them over and holds them all (`npm run exercise -- status` shows what it holds).

- **The feed** (`modules/exercise/viewer/ExerciseSection.tsx`): a card an activity, newest first: its sport's icon, when ("Today at 7:15 am", "Wednesday, 14 Oct 2026 at …") and what recorded it, its name, as a log names one its owner has not, by the part of the day it started in and its sport ("Morning Run", "Lunch Ride", "Evening Walk"; `titleOf`), its distance, pace (speed on a bike, a 100 m in the water), moving time and average heart rate, its route's thumbnail where it has one (on its area's map when one is kept here, see below), and its source, credited, with every other source that has it. The whole card opens its page.
- **The zone 2 target** (`modules/exercise/viewer/Zone2.tsx`), first, since the weekly zone 2 target lives here, counted from every workout by itself, rather than in Rituals: this week's minutes in zone 2, Monday to Sunday local time, large, over a bar filling to the target, with what is left (or Met) and the days left, and what they were counted from; beside it the last twelve weeks' minutes as bars under a dashed line at the target, a week that met it darker. Beside that card, at half the width each (stacked on a phone), **the gym sessions** (`modules/exercise/viewer/Gym.tsx`): this week's count, and each of the same twelve weeks' sessions as icons, one a session (workouts logged in Rituals under a gym category: push, pull and legs each their own icon, any other gym type a dumbbell), each naming its day and type on hover. Both charts share one frame (`modules/exercise/viewer/WeekChart.tsx`): pointing at (or tapping) a week shades it and puts the week and its total in the chart's heading, and the axis names each month at its first week. The target is one setting of the archive, `data/modules/exercise/exercise.json`'s `zone2WeeklyMinutes` (`data/exercise.json` in an archive whose file has not been moved yet; data.md's "Backups") (`{"zone2WeeklyMinutes": 150}`, `modules/exercise/server/settings.ts`), read with every answer; with none the page counts against nothing and says where to set it. Each activity's minutes in zone 2 (its `zone2`, `modules/exercise/server/rituals.ts`'s `zone2Of`) are counted once, whatever its copies: its trusted heart rate's zone 2 time when it has zones (counted here, see "Zones, counted here" below), else, with none, its Rituals log's stated minutes when the log says it was zone 2 and no other zone ("44 minutes, zone 2"; "zone 2-3" is not). A watch's heart rate, which is not trusted, counts nothing; a log on the same workout as a chest strap's recording adds nothing to the strap's minutes. Rituals' own Zone 2 target and its entries stay as they are for now; this page does not read them.
- **The weekly summary**: this week's distance, moving time and activities (weeks from Monday, local days) and how many in all as KPIs; beside the feed the last twelve weeks' distance as bars, and how many of each sport.
- **An activity's page**: its numbers (distance, moving time, pace or speed, elapsed time and how long stopped, average and maximum heart rate), its route large on a map of its area to move and zoom (or its shape with a scale bar where there is none), its splits a kilometre at a time (moving time, a bar as long as how fast against the others, and the split's average heart rate), its heart rate over time (the average dashed across, the reading under the pointer) and its time in each heart-rate zone (five, the hardest at the top, saying whose zones they are), its Rituals log when it has one (Rituals' title and category over the log's words, gym sets a line an exercise, each set a chip: "Bench press: 20 kg x 8; 40 kg x 10"), its minutes in zone 2 and what says so, where it came from (the source shown, every other source that has it, Rituals among them, and its heart rate's), and its Delete (below). Its address is the view's with `?activity=<id>`, so a reload, a link or Back opens it again.

**Every source, one activity each** (`modules/exercise/server/activities.ts`). The activities are the module's index's workouts (see events.md's "Workouts from Polar": the polar connector's copies and polar-export's) with by-hand.json's runs, each a copy like any other (its heart rate a summary, trusted as the file says), matched and ranked as "One activity, many copies" says, and the workouts logged in Rituals (the rituals module's `RitualWorkouts`, read from rituals' store). Of each: the card's facts are the copy shown's; its route, moving time and splits are the first copy by that order with distance over time or a route; its heart rate is the first copy by that order whose heart rate the captain trusts and that holds one. None trusted (a watch's, or a source with no rule yet), the activity is still shown, with its route and splits, and says its heart rate is not shown; a split's heart rate is shown only when the split was read from the trusted copy. So a run by hand gives way to Polar's copy of it once that is in and trusted, and until the captain sets Polar's trust rule the runs by hand keep their heart rate and Polar's copies show none.

**Zones, counted here** (`modules/exercise/server/zones.ts`). Polar works an activity's zones out from a maximum heart rate in its own settings, which the archive cannot change, so the archive counts them itself: `exercise.json`'s `heartRateZones` sets where zones 2 to 5 start, in bpm, and where they came from (`{"source": "…", "zone2": …, "zone3": …, "zone4": …, "zone5": …}`, from a physical assessment's zones, say; a new assessment is that one edit, read with every answer). Each activity's time in each zone is counted from its trusted heart rate's samples: each reading stands for its stream's interval (a second, in Polar's copies) at its heart rate, a gap in the stream (a strap that dropped out) stands for nothing, so no time goes to the zone beside it, and zone 1 is everything under zone 2's start, rest included. Those zones are the ones the activity shows (its zone line and its page's bars) and the ones its zone 2 minutes and its minutes in zones 4 and 5 are counted from, on Fitness's Activities and Bryan Johnson tabs. An activity whose trusted heart rate has no samples (a run transcribed by hand, a summary alone) keeps Polar's zones, as does every activity while `exercise.json` sets none (or sets them wrong: four rising numbers above 0); a Rituals log saying zone 2 counts as before. Both pages say whose zones they count by, and an activity's page says whose its are.

**A workout logged in Rituals** (`modules/exercise/server/rituals.ts`; the rituals module offers them as the SDK's `RitualWorkouts` service, `modules/rituals/server/service.ts`, read from its store at each ask, so with rituals off there are none) is laid on the activity it is, as another copy of it, when another source has it: Rituals keeps no time of day, so it must be of the same day (the activity's start) and the same family of sport (bike a ride, run a run, walk a walk; gym push, pull and legs none of those, so Polar's other sports, strength training among them), and as long, the minutes it states within 10 minutes or a quarter of them, whichever is more, of the activity's elapsed or moving time; a log that states no duration only when it is the day's one log of its family and the day has one such activity, so nothing is guessed between two. Each log is one activity's and each activity has one log at most, the closest pairs first. One matched to none is an activity of its own (its address `rituals-<day>-<its place in the day>`), known by its day alone ("Sunday, 1 Nov 2026", no time), named by Rituals' title, its card the log's first lines in place of distance and pace. Neither is deleted here: Rituals keeps its workouts, so the page says to take it out there first (the page reads the store afresh), and then whatever else of it is here can be deleted.

**What is worked out** (`modules/exercise/server/track.ts`), from the copy's own samples, in either of Polar's shapes: distance over time (its distance samples, else added up along the route where its points have times), the time moving (each stretch between two samples counts when it covered ground at least at a walk's pace on foot, a slow roll on a bike, and no stretch over 30 s, a gap in the recording, counts at all; the elapsed time when nothing says), the splits from the same, heart rate over time thinned to 600 points for the chart, and the route's shape. Each copy is read once and what was worked out kept for as long as the server runs (one store for the Fitness page and the Health tab), since a copy is written once and never changed; a delete forgets it all. AccessLink's copies spell their keys with underscores (`recording_rate`, `sample_type`, `lower_limit`, `in_zone`) where Polar's documentation has dashes: both are read (`modules/exercise/connectors/polar/exercise.ts`).

**Routes, on a map kept here.** A route is content (the archive's privacy policy, `docs/privacy.md`): it says where the owner went. A map's tiles fetched from a tile server would tell that server which area they were looking at each time, so the archive keeps its own maps instead, downloaded once (see "The maps" below), and the page reads them from the archive's server alone: viewing a workout never contacts a map server. A route leaves the server as a shape, each point in whole metres east and north of its own south-west corner, thinned (Douglas-Peucker) to what a drawing needs; when one of the maps kept here holds all of it, the answer also says which (`map`: the region, its highest zoom and that corner in degrees; `placeOf` in `modules/exercise/server/activities.ts`), and the page lays the shape on it (`modules/exercise/viewer/place.ts` undoes the flattening exactly). A route outside every map downloaded is drawn as before, on a plain dotted ground, saying its area's map is not downloaded yet, and the page is not told where it is. North up, start a filled dot and finish a ringed one, either way.

**The maps** (`modules/exercise/server/maps/`, `modules/exercise/viewer/RouteMap.tsx`). Each region's map is OpenStreetMap's data as vector tiles, cut from Protomaps' daily build (build.protomaps.com) to the region's box and kept in one PMTiles file, `data/maps/<region>.pmtiles`, with the fonts its labels are drawn in in `data/maps/fonts/` (Protomaps' basemaps-assets):

```bash
npm run map -- download <region>    # a region of the settings' maps.regions, once: about 134 MB of map and 11 MB of fonts, under a minute, then its terrain
npm run map -- terrain <region>     # its terrain alone (Greater Melbourne's is about 44 MB), for a region downloaded before terrain came
npm run map -- list                 # the regions there are, which are downloaded (and their terrain), how big and covering what
```

Each region's terrain is Mapterhorn's open terrain (mapterhorn.com: Terrarium-encoded WebP tiles of 512 pixels, to zoom 12, about 15 m a pixel at Melbourne, built from open elevation data, Copernicus GLO-30 and national surveys such as Geoscience Australia's; credited "© Mapterhorn" with a link to its list of sources), cut to the region's box from its whole-earth file the same way (Mapterhorn's data-access page gives `pmtiles extract` as the way to take an area) into `data/maps/terrain/<region>.pmtiles`, and served at `GET /life-archive/api/m/exercise/map/terrain/<region>/<z>/<x>/<y>.webp`. Only the playback in 3D (below) draws it.

`download` needs go-pmtiles (`brew install pmtiles`), whose `pmtiles extract` fetches only the region's tiles from the build; it writes beside the old file and replaces it only once whole, then fetches any font file missing. The regions are `maps.regions` in the settings, each cut from zoom 0 to 15; another is one entry there and a download. That download is the only time the maps reach the network. The server opens each file at the first request for it (again when it is downloaded anew; no restart) and answers `GET /life-archive/api/m/exercise/map/<region>/<z>/<x>/<y>.mvt` (a tile; 204 where the map has none) and `GET /life-archive/api/m/exercise/map/fonts/<font>/<range>.pbf`, the page's own only, reads only (`modules/exercise/server/maps/http.ts`, `modules/exercise/wire.ts`). The page draws them with MapLibre GL (loaded only when a map is first drawn, its worker from the server's own copy of the package) in a quiet style of Protomaps' basemap, near-white or near-black in the console's two looks, its points of interest, road shields and arrows left out, so it needs no icons (`modules/exercise/viewer/mapStyle.ts`). The feed's thumbnails are pictures of the map, each painted to fit its card by one map kept off the page once the card is near the screen (`modules/exercise/viewer/maplibre.ts`; a page of live maps would need a WebGL context each), the route's SVG over it; an activity's page has a live map, moved by dragging and zoomed with ⌘ or Ctrl and the wheel (two fingers by touch), with a scale. Each credits OpenStreetMap's contributors, as its licence (ODbL) asks.

**Playback in 3D** (`modules/exercise/viewer/playback/`, `modules/exercise/server/playback.ts`; after Strava's own playback of a journey, done better). An activity whose route lies inside a map kept here has Play back in 3D over its route; it opens full screen (the address gains `&play=1`, so Back closes it), on a phone too. A camera behind and above a marker follows it along the route, looking the way of travel, the trail bright behind it and the rest of the route dim ahead; play and pause (space), 1x, 2x, 4x and 8x of the time it took, Flyover (the whole route in 45 seconds, from higher and wider), a scrubber (← and → step ten seconds), and the moment's time, distance, speed (pace on foot), trusted heart rate with its zone (by the activity's zones' lower limits, zone 1's from 0; none more than 30 s from a reading, a strap's dropout), and elevation (the recording's altitude, else the terrain's). Its motion (`timeline.ts`, `camera.ts`): a pause of the recorder (a gap over 30 s) is cut to a second, a stop it recorded is played as it was with the marker held still; the route is smoothed (a Gaussian over a few seconds' points) and the marker moves along the drawn line by a monotone cubic of distance over time, so it glides and never runs back; the camera keeps the marker at the middle of its view and eases its turns. `GET /life-archive/api/m/exercise/activities/<id>/playback` sends the route whole (each timed point's time, place, distance so far and altitude), the trusted heart rate sample by sample (none when not trusted), the zones, and whether there is terrain and the photorealistic city's credentials; 409 for a route outside every map kept here, which the page is never told the place of. Two renderers draw it:

- **This Mac's 3D** (`localScene.ts`), always: MapLibre over the region's map with every OpenStreetMap building raised to its height (Protomaps' `height`, from OSM's height or levels; 6 m where it gives none), the terrain with a light relief, a sky, and the route draped on the ground, the trail's end exactly at the marker. It asks no server but this one.
- **The photoreal city** (`photorealScene.ts`), when `.env.local` sets `CESIUM_ION_TOKEN` (a Cesium ion account's; its Community plan carries Google's Photorealistic 3D Tiles), or `GOOGLE_MAPS_API_KEY` (Google's Map Tiles API directly; the token is used when both are set): CesiumJS, served from this server's copy of its build (`GET /life-archive/api/m/exercise/cesium/...`, `modules/exercise/server/cesium.ts`), draws Google's city with the route laid at the terrain's height, moved by the difference measured on the city at the start, drawn through anything in front of it. It is never the playback's first look: a playback opens over This Mac's 3D, with the city a button away when there are credentials, chosen each time (`scene.ts`'s `sceneKinds`); it falls back to This Mac's 3D, saying why, when the city cannot be had (a token refused at that address, say). The tileset is asked for once a playback and kept while it is open, so one playback is one root request: one ion session, good for three hours of tiles. The page asks Cesium ion for the tileset's address and Google for the tiles the camera sees, and as the camera follows the route those requests retrace it, so Google can learn where the route went, never its times, distance or heart rate (the module's privacy lines). The credentials are sent to the page itself alone, as every route's answers are.

`&record=1` in the playback's address stops its clock for a capture: `window.__playback.step(dt)` moves it one frame and `seek(at)` jumps, so a clip can be taken frame by frame.

**The routes**: `GET /life-archive/api/m/exercise/activities` (`modules/exercise/server/http.ts`, `modules/exercise/wire.ts`) answers every activity as its card (the route a thumbnail's worth of points), and `GET /life-archive/api/m/exercise/activities/<id>` one activity in full; under `/life-archive/`, so Tailscale Serve passes them on to the tailnet with the rest of the viewer. Each reads the module's index, which holds no content, the trust rules from each source's settings, and the copies from the archive's folder; each refuses a Host the door does not admit, a request from another site's page, and anything but a read or an activity's delete. The tests and the testing data use made-up workouts only (`modules/exercise/fixtures/polar.ts`: loops around 0°, 0°, in the sea), and `npm run testing-data` adds some in the five weeks to the day it runs, looping a few kilometres around the middle of the testing settings' region, so a testing folder's feed and weekly summary have something in them, and with that region's map in its `maps/` (`npm run map -- download sample-city` with `LIFE_ARCHIVE_DATA` naming it) draws them on it; the older ones stay in the sea, outside every map. The maps' tests use a made-up PMTiles file (`modules/exercise/fixtures/pmtiles.ts`).

**Bryan Johnson** (`/life-archive/dev/fitness/bryan-johnson`, `modules/exercise/viewer/Pillars.tsx`), Fitness's second tab: Bryan Johnson's five pillars of exercise, from his "Everyone Is Wrong About Exercise", each a bar chart of its weeks, so each pillar's progress reads at a glance. It was the Don't Die page, a spider graph of the week beside Fitness, until November 2026; its address (`/life-archive/dev/dont-die`) opens this tab. Above the cards is the captain's own picture of the five pillars as standing stones under a night sky (`modules/exercise/viewer/pillars-stones-*.webp`), a banner framed as the cards are: the whole picture on a phone, a band through the carvings on a wider screen. Each pillar is worked out in the page (`modules/exercise/viewer/pillars.ts`) from what the server already sends:

- **Strength Training**: the gym sessions logged in Rituals (as Activities counts them), against `liftWeeklySessions` in `exercise.json`;
- **Low Intensity Cardio**: the minutes in zone 2, against `zone2WeeklyMinutes`, Activities' zone 2 target;
- **High Intensity Cardio**: the minutes in heart-rate zones 4 and 5 by a trusted recording (the zones counted here), against `hiitWeeklyMinutes`;
- **Mobility & Flexibility**: the days the stretching ritual was done, of those reported, against every day;
- **Balance**: balance work, of which the archive logs none yet, so it has no bars, never a zero ("No balance logged yet"), against every day; planks never count as balance.

Each card says only this week's value against its target, beside a bar a Monday-to-Sunday week, this week at the left and older weeks to its right, back to the first week anything was counted, under a dashed line at the weekly target, in the Fitness page's own charts (`WeekChart.tsx`). This week, still in progress, is hatched; a week met is stronger than one short; a week with nothing reported has no bar; pointing at a week (tapping, on a phone) names it and its reading. A history longer than the card holds at 6px a week scrolls, opened at this week. (Activities' charts still run oldest at the left.) It reads the activities and `GET /life-archive/api/m/exercise/pillars`: the three targets, and each ritual day's stretching statuses (statuses alone, from the rituals module's `RitualHabits` service; none with that module off).


**Deleting an activity**, where delete means it is completely gone, not even a backup of it kept. At the foot of an activity's page, Delete this activity asks once, naming it by its date, sport and distance and saying what goes (`modules/exercise/viewer/DeleteActivity.tsx`; Cancel has the focus), and `DELETE /life-archive/api/m/exercise/activities/<id>` with the activity's start (so a page left open since deletes nothing else; the page's own only, with its Origin and a JSON body) removes every copy of it (`modules/exercise/server/delete.ts`):

- every copy's files: AccessLink's `<id>.json` and `<id>.fit` in `platform-exports/polar/accesslink/`, the export's `training-session_*.json`, and its entry in each zip kept beside the export (the zip written again without it, through a temporary file, every other entry's bytes as they were; a zip stored inside one likewise);
- every copy's rows in the calendar and then in the module's index (`workout` in its database), with SQLite's `secure_delete` on so their bytes are overwritten, and each WAL checkpointed;
- what the server had worked out of them.

Between the module's landing and the drop of the archive's old rows (`migrate friend-map-module`, the friend map step's move, which drops them), those rows and the archive's 48-hour backups still hold a deleted workout's index row: metadata only, no route or heart rate; the drop overwrites them for good.

No backup is taken first, as every change to people takes one: that would keep it. What stays is a tombstone a copy (`deleted_workout` in the module's database, `modules/exercise/server/deletedWorkouts.ts`): its source, the source's id, its sport family, when it started and ended, and when it was deleted, nothing of what it held. So no source brings it back: the import writer writes no copy that is a tombstone's own or the same activity as one (as "One activity, many copies" matches them), counting it (tally `workouts-deleted`); the host hands every connector the tombstones in its settings (`deletedWorkouts`, added as it runs, never written to its file), so the polar connector never fetches a deleted exercise by its id and keeps none it fetched that is the same activity, and polar-export reads none from any export, a later download included; and the live polar sync is stopped while a delete runs and started again with the new tombstones, so a pass under way cannot fetch one again.

It is refused, with nothing changed, when it cannot be whole: a run transcribed by hand (by-hand.json is the owner's own file; the page says so in place of the button), any activity while those runs cannot be known (the health module off, or by-hand.json not readable: a copy may be among them; the page says so too), a copy whose file holds another workout too (an export's session of several sports), a file outside `platform-exports/`, or a zip over 1 GiB. It cannot reach what holds a copy outside the module's database and the exports: the archive's own `workout` rows from before the module came, until `migrate friend-map-module` (the friend map step's move) drops them, and the backups of `archive.sqlite` from before that (`data/backups/`, each kept up to 48 hours), which hold those rows, never its files (the module's database has no copies of its own but the NAS's and Time Machine's); the NAS's nightly snapshots (a week) and Time Machine hold its files and rows; Polar Flow keeps it until it is deleted there (a later account export brings its file again, which the import leaves out); and any other service Polar shares it with (Strava, Apple Health).

## Journal

The owner's journals, notes and drafts can be an Obsidian vault, `data/Vault` (a link into iCloud Drive; see data.md's "Where the data lives"), edited in Obsidian on the Mac and the phone, and read in the archive only on the Journal page (below), a day at a time as it is opened. **It is a module** (`modules/journal/`, its README, and "Modules"; the third after health and exercise, and the first with a job outside the server; it had no page until 9 November 2026): its job is the voice journals' transcription below, the only place this project's code writes into the vault, with its command, `npm run journal`, and the launchd agent and launcher that command installs; its ledger of the recordings done is `modules/journal/ledger.json` in the archive's folder (taken over once from `voice-journal/ledger.json`, where it was before the journal was a module); its routes are the Journal page's, which only read; and its lines of the privacy policy say the journals are content, the most private the archive keeps (`docs/privacy.md`). It has no database and no rows in the calendar. The transcribers it uses (OpenSuperWhisper and Nemotron, `server/voiceTools.ts`) are the core's, shared with any module that needs them, reached through the SDK. Switch it off with `"modules": {"journal": false}` in the archive's `settings.json`: the core then loads nothing of it, and the Journal page leaves the rail; the launchd agent, launchd's rather than the server's, runs until `npm run journal -- uninstall`. A life archive without the module has no agent and nothing to switch off, since only its command installs one.

### The Journal page

The captain asked for "a journal page that's kind of like Obsidian ... Let's start with just the journals", and chose A · Notebook of three directions firstmate drew ("lets go with A"). Its first step is built (docs/console.md's "Journal"): second in the rail after Events, read-only, on the desk and the phone, the days, a day drawn from its Markdown, and beside it its outline, its sources (each recording playing and seeking, both transcripts, a source the journal leaves out said so) and the cleaner's edits. Obsidian stays the editor: "Open in Obsidian" on the desk, "Edit in Obsidian" on the phone, which the captain chose to keep read-only. The page reads the vault through the journal module's routes alone (`modules/journal/server/http.ts`), only inside `Journals/`: the list of days is names and sizes, not logged; a day, a transcript or edit list and a recording or picture are read only as he opens them, each kept in the trail of content reads as a viewer read (`npm run archive -- content-log`), for the console's own page alone, and never handed to a model (his call: "logged reads on open, no model"). Editing in place on the desk, with a guard against a clash with Obsidian or iCloud, is the next step; Search, a Year tab and a To check list come after.

### Voice journals, transcribed at 6 AM

Each recording Voice Memos syncs from the owner's iPhone is transcribed on this Mac and left ready for firstmate, the agent that processes the journals and writes each day's cleaned `Journals/<date>.md`: `npm run journal` (the journal module's command, `modules/journal/cli.ts`, and its job, `modules/journal/jobs/voice-journal/`), run by the launchd agent `local.life-archive-voice-journal` once a day at 06:00 (launchd runs it on wake when the Mac slept through 06:00), over everything Voice Memos has synced to `~/Library/Group Containers/group.com.apple.VoiceMemos.shared/Recordings` since the last pass, with a note to firstmate for each recording done in it, so filing follows straight after. Once a day rather than at each new recording, so that a recording made by mistake can be deleted before the pass gets to it. Nothing leaves the Mac: no upload, no cloud service.

- **Which recordings.** Every recording made since the agent's ledger began (`data/modules/journal/ledger.json`, made by its first pass), each once, keyed by its file name; earlier ones are left alone, and one whose `journal-source-v1` record is already in `Journals/raw/` (firstmate's by hand) is marked done. All of them, not only journals: Voice Memos holds other recordings too (an appointment, a talk), and firstmate decides what each is. One deleted in Voice Memos is never taken, though its file stays in the folder: Voice Memos keeps a deleted recording's file while it is in Recently Deleted, marking its row in its index (`CloudRecordings.db`, read-only) with the time it was deleted (`ZEVICTIONDATE`), and drops the row when it erases it for good, so a file the index does not list is left alone too; one restored from Recently Deleted is taken at the next pass. When the index is there but cannot be read (Voice Memos writing it, say), a pass takes nothing (nothing more, when it is part way), since it cannot tell which were deleted, and notes firstmate once that day; the next pass tries again. A recording is taken only once it has arrived: unchanged for a minute, its audio readable to the end (Voice Memos writes the index of a recording's sound last, so a file cut short cannot be read through), listed in Voice Memos' index (for its start, length and title), and as long as the index says. One still arriving is looked at every half minute for ten minutes, then at the next pass (one the index did not list at an earlier pass either is not waited on: its file is a deleted recording's); one unchanged for six hours that is still shorter than the index says is taken, its doubt written in its record.
- **What it writes**, in `data/Vault/Journals/`, for a recording begun at 01:44 on 2 November (before 06:00, so the 1 November journal's, as the journal rules say): its copy, byte for byte and checked by SHA-256, at `assets/2026-11-01/voice-memos/20261102-014448-AAAA1111.m4a` (the original is only read); OpenSuperWhisper's transcript, `raw/2026-11-01/0144-voice-transcript-osw.md`; Nemotron's, `0144-voice-transcript-nemotron.md`; and the `journal-source-v1` record, `0144-voice.source.json`, written last (with no record, a recording is not done, and the next pass does it again). The names and the record's shape are firstmate's journal format; a second recording in the same minute (a false start) is `0144-voice-2`. The record's `raw_file` is Nemotron's transcript when it came back punctuated, else OpenSuperWhisper's; the edits file, `cleanup_sha256` and the ambiguous words are firstmate's to add.
- **How it is transcribed.** OpenSuperWhisper (the installed app, on Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3; a pass refuses it when it is set to Whisper, which dropped long stretches of a test recording) is handed the copy with `open -g -a OpenSuperWhisper`, and its transcript is read from its history (`~/Library/Application Support/ru.starmel.OpenSuperWhisper/recordings.sqlite`, read-only, the entry for that file), waiting up to ten minutes and a quarter of the recording's length. As OpenSuperWhisper does with any file, it keeps its own copy of the audio in its recordings folder and the transcript in its history. Then Nemotron 3.5 Transcribe 30B (`NEMOTRON_MODEL` in `.env.local`, its encoder beside it) through NeMo-Speech.cpp (`~/.cache/voice-journal-transcriber-eval/nemo-speech/`), from a 16 kHz mono copy ffmpeg makes in a temporary folder, with the bake-off's instruction; when it comes back unpunctuated (it did once in four files there) it is asked once more in other words. When the model (about 20 GB) or the runner is not on this Mac that step is skipped, and the record and the note say so; when it fails, OpenSuperWhisper's transcript stands alone. The transcribers are the core's (`server/voiceTools.ts`), which the module reaches through the SDK.
- **Telling firstmate.** A note through firstmate's inbox for each recording done (`bin/fm-inbox.sh note --request-id voice-journal-<recording>`, so never twice): the journal day, the raw files and the audio, and how Nemotron went. A recording that fails is tried again at the next pass (the next morning's, or one `launchctl kickstart gui/$(id -u)/local.life-archive-voice-journal` runs once 15 minutes have passed), three times, and then firstmate is told why (at once when trying again cannot help, OpenSuperWhisper set to Whisper, say); `npm run journal -- retry "<file>"` tries it again once fixed.
- **Full Disk Access.** macOS lets a process list Voice Memos' folder only with Full Disk Access (checked on this Mac: a launchd job without it is refused with "Operation not permitted", and asked nothing, so the smaller "data from other apps" permission does not cover it). As for mac-imports, the agent runs a launcher of its own (`server/launcher.c`, built for `modules/journal/cli.ts`) at `~/Library/Application Support/LifeArchive/life-archive-voice-journal`, and the grant goes to it alone, not to node. The vault is in iCloud Drive, so the first time it writes there macOS may ask whether it may "access files managed by iCloud Drive" (see data.md's "Where the data lives"). Until the grant is given, a pass does nothing and notes firstmate once that day, saying what to grant; recordings made meanwhile are done once it is. Sharing each memo to an iCloud Drive folder from the phone instead would need no Full Disk Access, but it is a step by hand each time, and iCloud Drive asks a permission of its own anyway.
- **The captain's part, once.** System Settings > Privacy & Security > Full Disk Access; click +, press Cmd-Shift-G, paste `~/Library/Application Support/LifeArchive/life-archive-voice-journal`, choose Open, and check its switch is on; then Allow, if macOS asks about iCloud Drive at the first recording. **Rows of dots** (`modules/rituals/viewer/dots.ts`, `DotRows.tsx`, `dots.css`), Fitness's Habits' (Daily rituals draws its days as a run of its own, below), small dots rather than a row for each entry: a weekly target a row, its name and how many of its counted weeks were met held at the left ("5/7"), then a dot a week, oldest at the left, with a month named over its first week. Each row is named at its newest end, the left, with how many of its counted days were done, so a habit or check that began lately has its ticks and its name beside today and its empty days before it began at the right. A day it was not asked (rituals' not-applicable days, such as the plank's, or a check not asked that day) is a grey dot of a tick's own size ("Not asked should get a grayed out dot"), and it is not counted. A closed day with no answer is a not done ("On daily rituals no answer is considered not done"): the same red tick, counted as not done in the row's count, its tooltip "Not done (no answer)", where rituals' own rates take a missing day the same way. Wind-down, a daily ritual from 9 November 2026, is judged by rituals against midnight at the end of its day: green when its first known time is before it, red when at or after it (still counted as done, since the day closes at 06:00 like any other), red too for a closed day without it and for today's once midnight has passed with no answer (before then it is still going, and it can still be ticked until 06:00), and a neutral tick (the muted grey, darker than a not-asked day's) when it was ticked with no time; its tooltip gives the time and how it is known ("Done · 23:40 as stated, before midnight"). Today is never counted ("Let's also not count today. The axis names the newest day shown at its left edge and the Mondays there is room for, each held to the right edge of its Monday, where its week begins and its seam is. A day is never narrower than 3 px: when the whole run would be (a phone after about three months, a desk after about ten), the most recent days that fit are shown, the count is over those, and each row's count says since when. There is no legend under the card ("Don't need the legend"); each tick's hover says what it is. A target counted in fish is left out of the tab (its week's met count over the rest): rituals still keeps and counts it, the view only does not show it.

Both ask again when they open, when the tab comes back into view and each minute in view; when an ask fails they keep what they had and say so. Neither shows the evening's check-in time on its own (only in journalling's tooltips), the workouts (Fitness's Activities has them) or a target's entries.

**Before the dots** the Rituals page had three tabs, Daily rituals, Weekly targets and Previous. No habit time, note or anything else of the view is sent.

**Which rituals.** The store in the archive's folder, `modules/rituals/data/ritual.sqlite3`. With none there each tab says rituals is not set up (and `npm run rituals -- status` says where ritualctl would make it); when rituals cannot read it (a broken file) it says rituals answered with an error, in rituals' own words. ## Inspirations

The captain's inspirations: the posts, reels, videos, pages and photos he keeps for the ideas in them, sorted by what they are about, so that one thing can be about two at once (cooking and art). Each is one item in the inspirations module's database (`modules/inspirations/`, its README: a page of the core's until 8 November 2026, whose rows, ids and change history it took over once from `core.sqlite`; `inspiration`), holding his caption (his own words about it: how it makes him feel, why he kept it) and his tags, fed by any number of saves (`inspiration_save`: each time and place it was sent in, kept as it came: its origin, the link as given, the words with it, and when). `modules/inspirations/server/store.ts` is the only code that reads or writes their tables.

- **One item for one thing.** Every link is reduced to one canonical form before it is matched (`modules/inspirations/server/links.ts`): Instagram's `/p/<code>/`, `/reel/<code>/`, `/reels/` and `/tv/` (also under a user's path) are `https://www.instagram.com/p/<code>/`, the form a reel when it was one; X and Twitter status links are `https://x.com/i/status/<id>`; `youtu.be/<id>`, `/watch?v=`, `/shorts/`, `/embed/` and `/live/` are `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=<id>`, a short still a short; any other link loses its fragment, its tracking (`utm_*`, `fbclid`, `igsh`, `si` and the like) and its trailing slash. So the reel he sends today and the same reel sent again next month are one item with two saves: the second's words are added to the caption (a blank line between, never twice), its tags to the item's, and what it says of the thing fills only what is still unknown. A photo or screenshot with no link is an item of its own, joined by its content hash.
- **What is known of it**: its title, author and description, from what the agent that sent it saw (`--title`, `--author`, `--description`), then from its preview, which only fills what is still empty: YouTube's and X's public oEmbed (title, channel and thumbnail; the post's text and author), Instagram's public embed page, slowly (see "Instagram's previews" below), and any other page's own meta tags (og:, twitter:, its `<title>`, its author), read from its first megabyte. Nothing behind a login is fetched, nor this Mac, the local network, a tailnet name or an address alone, even through a redirect (`modules/inspirations/server/previews.ts`).
- **Kept files.** A photo or screenshot sent in is copied into the module's folder, `data/modules/inspirations/files/<xx>/<sha256>.<ext>` (`inspiration_file`, role `item`, its path from the module's folder), by its hash as the live bridges keep their media, and the NAS backup takes it with the module's folder; so is the one small picture of an Instagram post (role `preview`), shown when the item has no photo of its own. Nothing else is downloaded: never a post's video, its full-size image or a carousel's others.
- **Two kinds of tags, never merged.** *His tags* are his alone, and optional: any number on an item, from his inspiration vocabulary (`inspiration_tag`, apart from people's tags), each with a line saying what it covers and in his order, written as people's tags are (`normalizeTag`: `Interior design` is `interior-design`). Tagging an item with a new one adds it to the vocabulary, last; a tag nobody has stays until he deletes it; renaming one onto another merges the two, as people's tags do. *AI tags* are made for every item by the archive's AI service (see queries.md's "The AI service"), from his caption and everything known of it (its title, author and description, as the agent that sent it saw them and as its preview found them), steered by his vocabulary (each tag with what it covers and how many have it) and the AI tags already in use: two to five free words, written as tags are, best first, kept apart (`inspiration_ai_tag`) with the model and when, and made again when his caption changes, when a preview lands after them with more of what is known (Instagram's, fetched slowly), or when he asks (Make again; `inspiration ai-tags`). They are never made his. Every list and query is filtered by his tags or by the AI's, never both at once, so he can try the AI's out against his own. Tags are the AI's job: his words are the caption, and he corrects a wrong tag rather than tagging by hand.
- **Privacy.** Inspirations are metadata, all of them: the link, what is known of it, his caption, both kinds of tags, the saves and the kept files. External models read them freely, with no logged content read (no `--why`, no trail), for version 1. The privacy policy says so too (its metadata layer, the module's line in `modules/inspirations/module.ts`).

**Capture: one way in.** Every door (the iPhone's share sheet, his Grok Bot agent, the terminal) goes through one function, `Inspirations.capture` (the CLI's `inspiration add`, and the server's capture route for the share sheet), which reduces the link, finds or makes the item, records the save, adds his words and tags, and leaves its preview and AI tags waiting for the archive's server. His words are kept verbatim: the item's caption is the first save's words as he gave them (trimmed, nothing else), and a later save's words are kept on that save and added after a blank line, never replacing what he wrote before.

```sh
npm run archive -- inspiration add "<link>" --caption "<his words>" [--tag music,art] \
  [--title "<what it is>"] [--author "<who made it>"] [--description "<what it shows>"] [--origin grok-bot] [--wait]
npm run archive -- inspiration add ~/Downloads/screenshot.png --caption "tiles for the bathroom"   # a photo, kept by its hash
```

A capture is no changeset (as an import is not, it takes no backup either), and it says the item's number, whether it is new or another save of one, and its AI tags: within a few seconds the server's worker (`modules/inspirations/server/worker.ts`, the module's work while the server runs) has fetched the preview and asked for them, and `--wait` waits for them (up to 90 s) and prints them, about 4 s on this Mac. An AI that cannot answer (Codex signed out, say) leaves them waiting, tried again after 1, 2, 4… minutes (at most 30), and given up on after six tries until asked again (`inspiration ai-tags --failed`).

**Instagram's previews.** Downloading the Instagram saves whole would risk the account, so instead each Instagram item keeps its link, its author, his note, the post's own words and one small picture, read from the post's public embed page (`https://www.instagram.com/p/<code>/embed/`, the page Instagram gives any site that embeds it), and the page shows the live embed only when he clicks the picture (`modules/inspirations/server/instagram.ts`):

- *Logged out, always.* No login, cookie, account or session; nothing of his. The page carries the post (its `contextJSON`: the owner, the caption, the picture's sizes, else its HTML) only when asked as a browser asks for a page, with a browser's document headers; asked otherwise it is an empty shell. The picture kept is the post's smallest of 320 px or more across (about 20 to 50 KB), from Instagram's own picture servers only, refused over 400 KB; `check-data` names any preview picture over 400 KB. The import's placeholder title (`Instagram save <code>`) goes once its post is found, and the card is named by the post's first line.
- *Slowly.* The server's worker takes one post at a time, 15 to 30 s apart, its picture a few seconds after its page, at most 100 posts in any 24 hours. When Instagram refuses one (429, 401, 403, its login page, a challenge), or five posts in a row show nothing, it stops for 6 hours, twice that each time in a row, up to two days, then goes on with the same post. Every fetch is recorded (`inspiration_fetch`), so the pace holds across restarts.
- *Each item says where it stands* (`preview_note`, shown in its panel and by `inspiration show`): found; no picture and why; a post whose page shows nothing, tried twice more a day apart and then skipped (removed, private, or not to be embedded); a fetch that failed, tried again after 1, 2, 4 hours and given up on after four; or blocked, with when it goes on. An item with no picture still shows its link, and its embed on click.
- *Started by him, not by a merge.* A new Instagram capture is asked for at once (its AI tags never wait for it); the items imported before are asked for only once he starts the back-fill:

```sh
npm run archive -- inspiration instagram          # how they stand: found, waiting, skipped, given up on; the day's fetches; the next
npm run archive -- inspiration instagram start    # ask for every Instagram item without a picture (at 100 a day, 500 saves take about five days)
npm run archive -- inspiration instagram stop     # ask for none still waiting (start asks again)
```

Neither is a changeset (a preview is not his edit), and the server must be running: it fetches, the command only asks.

**From the iPhone's share sheet.** The quickest door, for what he comes across scrolling: the **Inspire** shortcut sits in the share sheet of Safari, Instagram, YouTube, Photos and any app that shares a link, text or a picture. Share, say or type a line about it (the keyboard's microphone dictates), done: it posts the link (or the shared text, or the photo) and his words to the archive's capture route, `POST /life-archive/api/inspirations/capture` (`modules/inspirations/server/http.ts`), over the tailnet at `https://your-mac.tail1234.ts.net/life-archive/api/inspirations/capture` (Tailscale Serve sends `/life-archive` on to the archive's server; the phone must be on the tailnet), and shows "Saved #42 (new): …" as a notification, or the archive's error in an alert (a capture never fails silently). The route takes a form (`multipart/form-data`: `link`, `text`, `caption`, `origin`, `file`) or JSON (`link`, `text`, `caption`, `origin`): the link as given, or the first link in the shared text, or a shared photo kept by its hash, or shared text with no link kept as a text file (its first line the title, the text its description); his words are the caption, verbatim; the origin is `share-sheet` unless the caller says. It answers at once, the preview and the AI tags made by the worker in the seconds after. Who may post: the door's Host guard and the tailnet's ACL, as for every page; the route asks for no Origin, since a shortcut is not a page, and a page on another site is still refused by its browser's `Sec-Fetch-Site` or `Origin`. The signed shortcut and its one-screen install note are kept outside this repository; a share from the phone with no words still saves the thing, for words later.

**From a Grok Bot agent.** Where the owner sets one up, a Grok Bot agent on the Mac, logged in to whichever platforms he chooses, needs no MCP or server of its own: when he sends it something, it looks at it with its logins and runs the command above from the archive's checkout (with the pinned Node first on the PATH), with the link, his words as `--caption`, what it saw as `--title`, `--author` and `--description`, `--origin grok-bot` and `--wait`, and tells him the item's number and its AI tags. The instruction to paste into the agent's charter is `docs/grok-bot-inspirations.md`. X's saves come in the same way for now: the agent opens them logged in and sends each, rather than X's paid API. It is the second door, for talking a thing through; both doors are the same capture.

**A caption is only kept.** The archive houses an inspiration: his words are its caption and the AI reads them only to choose tags. A caption that asks for something to be built ("it'd be cool if we reproduced this", "build this") is kept and tagged like any other and sent to no one; nothing is prototyped from it, by the archive or by firstmate (the captain's call, 8 November 2026). Until then the archive's worker sent firstmate a probe request for such a caption; items from then may still carry the `probe_requested_at`, `probe_result` and `probe_result_at` it left, which stay readable (the page shows an old item's result link; `inspiration show` prints the probe) but nothing sets them, and the `inspiration probe` command is gone.

**The page** (the console's Inspirations, `/life-archive/dev/inspirations`, on this Mac and on the tailnet, the captain's phone included; `modules/inspirations/viewer/`): **Platforms** is how many platforms his inspirations come from and how many each gives (hidden ones aside, as every count here), a bar each, the most first; choosing one opens Recent narrowed to it; **Recent** is everything, newest first, or one platform's (its chip's × shows every platform's again), and Hidden instead behind a switch; **By tag** narrows to every tag picked (cooking + art), of his or, switched to AI tags, of the AI's; **Search** finds every word in his caption, the title, author, description, link and both kinds of tags; **Untagged** is what has none of his tags yet; **Tags** is his vocabulary (rename, onto another to merge; what each covers; up and down; delete, after a second press), with the AI tags in use under it; **Changes** is the change log's changesets of inspirations, each with Undo (Redo for an undo). Each list comes in pages of 48 (Newer, Older, and the pages by number), its page kept in the address. A card shows its picture first, and its link always under it. A card opens its item at the right (from the bottom on a phone): its picture (a kept file, else its preview's; an Instagram post's small picture, which loads the live embed from Instagram in its place only when clicked, no request going to Instagram before), a link out with the link written out, what is known of it, his words to edit (Save), his tags (× to take one off, a box to add more, his other tags offered), the AI's tags apart (with the model and when, and Make again), the probe an old item carries (when firstmate was told, and the result's link), every save (when, from where, with its words), and Hide or Show again. What a tab is narrowed to, its page and the item open are kept in the address. Every edit is one changeset, and its notice offers Undo at once. The look is the viewer's own and plain, while the viewer's redesign is worked out apart.

**Who may change them.** These are the viewer's first routes that write (`modules/inspirations/server/http.ts`, under `/life-archive/api/inspirations`), scoped to inspirations alone: an undo of a changeset that changed anything else is refused (409), to be undone in friend-map or with `changes undo`. A write is answered only to a Host the door admits, and only from the page itself (a JSON body, and an Origin naming this server, which a browser sends with every write); the capture route alone asks for no Origin (see "From the iPhone's share sheet"). There is no per-person login: on the tailnet, its ACL decides who reaches the viewer, so a device the tailnet admits may change inspirations as well as read them. Reading is the same: the tailnet's ACL and the door decide who reaches the viewer, as for every other page.

```sh
npm run archive -- inspiration list [--tag music,art | --ai-tag cooking] [--untagged] [--search tiles] [--hidden] [--json]
npm run archive -- inspiration show 12 [--json]       # everything known, his tags, the AI's (model, when), every save
npm run archive -- inspiration tag 12 music art       # untag the same way; a changeset each, with --why
npm run archive -- inspiration caption 12 "slow hands, and the wheel"
npm run archive -- inspiration hide 12                # unhide 12 shows it again
npm run archive -- inspiration ai-tags 12             # ask again (--failed: every one given up on)
npm run archive -- inspiration edit tag cooking --ai-tag cooking    # many at once: the dry run; --apply --expect <n> makes it
npm run archive -- inspiration-tag list               # his vocabulary in order, what each covers, counts; the AI tags in use
npm run archive -- inspiration-tag add "interior design" --description "rooms, tiles and furniture; not whole buildings"
npm run archive -- inspiration-tag rename rooms interior-design     # onto a tag that exists, the two are merged
npm run archive -- inspiration-tag describe craft "made by hand"    # move <tag> <position>, delete <tag>
```

Each edit of his (caption, tags, hidden, the vocabulary), from the page or the CLI, is one changeset in the change log (see people.md's "Changing data from a chat": the CLI's after a checked backup), shown with each item's before and after (`inspiration #12 "Throwing a tall vase": tags craft -> craft, art`) and undone with Undo or `changes undo`; a capture and the AI's tags are not his edits and make none. `npm run check-data` checks every item's link is in its canonical form, its caption written as a note is, that it has a save (and, with no link, a kept file), each save's origin, each kept file by its hash in `modules/inspirations/files/` and there, a preview's picture no more than 400 KB, both kinds of tags written as tags are, and an old item's probe result a web link. The testing data (`npm run testing-data`) has made-up inspirations, with two kept pictures drawn as stripes, and 52 made-up Instagram saves as the import and the slow previews leave them (most with a picture drawn as stripes, some not yet asked for, one skipped), enough for two pages; none of them is waiting, so a server on it never asks Instagram.

## Shopping

One place to see every supplier's specials, instead of going to each website with its own layout.

The shopping module (`modules/shopping/`, its README), unfinished: off in a fresh install until switched on (`"modules": {"shopping": true}`) or every page is (`"pages": "all"`). Its aspect, at `/life-archive/dev/shopping`, has one view so far, **On special**: every supplier's specials in one layout. It replaced a small price watcher (a Python project), keeping its supplier fetching and dropping its product matching. It was a part of the core until 8 November 2026, and kept its address (`/life-archive/api/shopping`) and its data's place in the archive's folder (`shopping/`) as it moved.

**What is pulled.** The suppliers in `shopping/suppliers.json` in the archive's folder (`modules/shopping/server/suppliers.ts` has the shape); with none there is no pull, and the page says where they go. Each is read through its storefront's public feed (`server/fetch.ts`, parsed in `parse.ts`):

- `shopify`: the collection's `products.json`, or, when that does not answer with JSON, the collection's page (its `ProductItem` cards);
- `shopify-storefront`: a headless Shopify storefront (a Nuxt site, whose `products.json` is gone). It is read through Shopify's Storefront API, at the checkout domain and with the public token its own page carries (`shopifyCheckoutDomain`, `shopifyStorefrontToken`), read afresh each pull;
- `woocommerce`: the Store API (`/wp-json/wc/store/v1`), the supplier's specials category when it has one (`specialsCategory`), else every product on sale. A variable product's sizes are read from its page's `data-product_variations`, named by the product's attribute terms;
- `wix`: the storefront's page, its product cards.

A special is an in-stock size whose price is below the price the supplier says it was (Shopify's compare-at, WooCommerce's regular, Wix's before-discount). Where a supplier's page lists only specials (`listsSpecials`, a WooCommerce specials category among them), a product there with no size marked down is still one: its cheapest in-stock size, shown as a "listed special" with no discount. Where it lists more (new products beside its specials, or the whole shop), only what is marked down counts. Each special has its supplier, name, size, link, picture, price, the price before, the discount (whole percent) and, when its weight is known (from the size, the name, or Shopify's shipping weight; or a name like "Brisket $19.99kg" priced by the kilogram), its price a kilogram.

A supplier is **working** when it was read and has at least one special; otherwise the page says why (its error, or that it lists nothing on special).

**When.** The module pulls while the archive's server runs (`server/pull.ts`: the `Shopping` service it offers, and its morning pulls, its `work`): each morning at 08:00 local time, once as the server starts when today has no pull and 08:00 has passed, and whenever the page's Refresh now asks (`POST /life-archive/api/shopping`, the page's own only). A pull asked for while one runs waits for that one. Each pull is kept whole in the archive's folder, `data/shopping/pulls/<UTC time>.json` (`ShoppingPull` in the module's `wire.ts`), so the history grows; nothing reads it but for the latest yet. Switched off, the module runs none of this: no pulls, its route answers 404 and its page is out of the rail (`server/moduleSwitch.test.ts`).

**The page** (the module's `viewer/ShoppingSection.tsx`, its arithmetic in `viewer/format.ts`): KPIs (products on special, suppliers working, the most off, when it was pulled and when next, and Refresh now); each supplier with its working dot, count, how many products it looked at, and why not when it is not working, each a filter; then a card a product (its sizes on special within it), found by words, sorted by the most off (or $/kg, price, name), and grouped by supplier or not.
