# The exercise module

The life archive's Fitness page (the console's `/life-archive/dev/fitness`; Exercise until 7 November 2026, its old address still opening it, the module's id still `exercise`), whose Activities and Bryan Johnson tabs are this module's (its Habits tab is the rituals module's): every workout from every source, one activity each, laid out as a training log (a feed newest first, the week's zone 2 against its target, the week's totals, and a page for each activity with its route on a map kept on this Mac, its trusted heart rate, its splits and its Rituals log), Bryan Johnson's five pillars of exercise, each a bar chart of its weeks against its weekly target (`/life-archive/dev/fitness/bryan-johnson`, once the Don't Die page beside Fitness, whose address opens it); and its Workouts table, which the Events page's Tables lists. The core's README says how it all works ("Exercise", "Workouts from Polar"), and how a module is laid out, found and switched (`docs/modules.md`).

It is the first module with a store of its own, the proof of the shape: a module that runs inside a life archive, never on its own, with its own database, its own connectors and importers, its rows in the core's calendar, and a job: the Polar live sync, which the core's host runs every 3 hours. The core loads `module.ts` (its server half) and `viewer.tsx` (its page half), and the module imports the core through the SDK alone (`../../sdk/`).

- `module.ts`: its database (`server/db.ts`), which the core opens and keeps; its connectors, `polar` (Polar's AccessLink, live, every 3 hours) and `polar-export` (Polar's account export), which the core's host runs as it runs its own, each import's events applied to the module's database (`server/apply.ts`) and the rows of what changed pushed into the core's calendar; its route (`GET /life-archive/api/m/exercise/activities`, `.../activities/<id>`, `.../activities/<id>/playback` for its playback in 3D, `DELETE .../activities/<id>`, `.../map/...` for the maps and their terrain, and `.../cesium/...` for CesiumJS's build); the `Exercise` service the health module asks for optionally (the highest heart rate a trusted recording holds); its answers for its calendar rows and every row for the calendar's rebuild (`server/calendar.ts`); the Workouts table (`server/workoutsTab.ts`), which the Events page's Tables lists; its made-up workouts for the testing data (`fixtures/polar.ts`); and its lines of the privacy policy (`server/privacy.ts`).
- `viewer.tsx`: the Fitness aspect and its own two views, Activities and Bryan Johnson, beside rituals' Habits (`viewer/ExerciseSection.tsx` and the files beside it: the drawings, the route on a map, the zone 2 and gym panels, the delete, and the playback in 3D in `viewer/playback/`: its timeline and camera, the archive's own 3D over the maps and terrain kept here (`localScene.ts`) and the photorealistic city through CesiumJS (`photorealScene.ts`)); and the five pillars, a bar chart each (`viewer/Pillars.tsx`, its arithmetic in `viewer/pillars.ts`, which the `exercise_pillars` tool shares, and its look in `viewer/pillars.css`), drawn by the same weekly chart as the zone 2 and gym panels (`viewer/WeekChart.tsx`).
- `wire.ts`: the answers the page draws, the module's addresses, and the page's address in the console, shared by both halves.
- `server/`: the index of workouts (`workoutIndex.ts`), which copies are one activity and which to show (`workouts.ts`), what the page shows of each (`activities.ts`, `track.ts`, `rituals.ts`, and `zones.ts`: its time in each heart-rate zone, counted from its samples by the zones `exercise.json` sets), its playback (`playback.ts`) and CesiumJS's build for it (`cesium.ts`), the delete (`delete.ts`), the tombstones (`deletedWorkouts.ts`), the Workouts table's editor (`workoutEdits.ts`) and the captain's corrections and notes it writes (`corrections.ts`, kept beside each copy, which every read of the index takes over it), the services (`service.ts`), the weekly targets and the heart-rate zones (`settings.ts`, `modules/exercise/exercise.json` in the archive's folder, or `exercise.json` at its root until `npm run archive -- migrate module-data` moves it: zone 2's minutes, the Bryan Johnson tab's gym sessions and minutes in zones 4 and 5, and where zones 2 to 5 start) and the maps kept on this Mac (`maps/`).
- `connectors/`: the two Polar connectors, written as the core's are (`../../sdk/connector.ts`).
- `cli.ts`: `npm run exercise -- status | polar login [--new-client] | map download <region> | map terrain <region> | map list` (`npm run map` is the last three).

**Its data** lives in the archive's folder: `modules/exercise/exercise.sqlite` (the index, the tombstones, the captain's corrections and notes with their change log, and the connectors' runs; `npm run archive -- backup` keeps a checked copy of it beside the archive's, and the NAS backup sends one each night, as it does the archive's and the calendar's), the copies the connectors keep in `platform-exports/polar/`, the maps and their terrain in `maps/`, the connectors' settings in `connectors/polar.json` and `connectors/polar-export.json`, and the weekly targets in `modules/exercise/exercise.json`. The index and the tombstones are metadata; a copy's route and heart rate are content, by the captain's word (`server/privacy.ts`). Switch the module off with `"modules": {"exercise": false}` in the archive's `settings.json`: the page, the Workouts tab, the two connectors and the maps go with it, and the rest of the archive is as it was.

**When the module came** (the first start of the server with it on), its database was new beside an archive that held its rows: it copied them in once, ids kept (`server/db.ts`'s take-over: the workout events with their detail, the tombstones, and the polar connectors' runs and cursors), so every activity kept its address and the live connector went on from where it was. The archive's rows from before stay until `npm run archive -- migrate friend-map-module --apply` (the friend map step's move, which drops them too) overwrites them for good, which is refused until the module's database has taken them over and holds them all (`npm run exercise -- status` shows what it holds). Until then, those rows and the archive's 48-hour backups still hold a deleted workout's index row, metadata only.
